## Where the price actually changes the rules

Most of what is written about luxury real estate is about finishes. The part that changes your numbers is statutory. There are three lines worth knowing before you start.

At **$1.5 million** insured financing stops being available. At **$2 million** Ontario adds a land transfer tax bracket. And for a foreign buyer, a 25 percent tax applies at any price. Everything else at this level is judgment. These three are arithmetic.

## The 2.5 percent land transfer tax bracket

Ontario's residential land transfer tax runs in brackets:

| Portion of the price | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to $55,000 | 0.5 percent |
| $55,001 to $250,000 | 1.0 percent |
| $250,001 to $400,000 | 1.5 percent |
| $400,001 to $2,000,000 | 2.0 percent |
| Above $2,000,000 | 2.5 percent |

The top bracket has a condition attached that catches people out. It applies to property containing **at least one and not more than two single family residences**. Where a property holds more than two. None, the maximum rate is 2 percent whatever the value.

Worked through, a $2,500,000 Markham home carries $48,975 in provincial land transfer tax. A $3,000,000 home carries $61,475.

Markham sits in York Region, so there is no municipal land transfer tax on top. The same purchase inside the City of Toronto would carry a second tax of comparable size. On a $2.5 million home that difference is substantial. It is one of the honest financial arguments for buying here. Our post on [land transfer tax in Markham](/blog/land-transfer-tax-markham/) sets out the full calculation.

## Financing above the insured cap

The insured mortgage price cap is $1.5 million. Below it, a buyer can put down less than 20 percent and insure the mortgage. Above it, that option does not exist.

So on a $2.5 million purchase the minimum down payment is $500,000. It is a conventional mortgage. Two practical consequences:

- **Start the financing conversation earlier.** Conventional approvals at this size involve more documentation and income that is self employed, corporate or partly outside Canada takes longer to verify.
- **Budget the closing costs separately.** On that same purchase, land transfer tax alone is $48,975 before legal fees, title insurance and adjustments.

## The non-resident speculation tax

Ontario's non-resident speculation tax has applied to residential property **anywhere in Ontario since October 25, 2022**, at **25 percent**.

Two details matter more than the rate. It applies to foreign nationals, foreign corporations and taxable trustees. And it is charged on the full value of the transaction where any single transferee is a foreign entity, rather than being prorated by ownership share. A purchase structured with one foreign party among several Canadian ones is taxed on the whole price.

There are rebates and exemptions in specific circumstances. Those are a question for a lawyer who handles them regularly, before the offer rather than after.

## Selling at this level is a different job

The thing that changes is not the marketing budget. It is the arithmetic of the buyer pool.

At a median price, a correctly priced home meets many potential buyers in its first weekend. At three times that price, the number of households who can transact at all is small and the right one arrives less often. That single fact drives everything else:

**Time on market is normal, not a verdict.** A quiet first three weeks at this level is not the signal it would be on a median home. Cutting the price in week three, before the market has seen a full cycle of buyers, usually costs more than waiting.

**Price to genuinely comparable evidence.** With few comparables, the temptation is to price on ambition. The market corrects that slowly and expensively. Where the evidence is thin, we say so rather than pretending to a precision that does not exist.

**Preparation carries more weight.** A buyer at this level is comparing your home with a small number of alternatives and looking closely. What is worth spending on is a conversation to have before listing.

**Decide about privacy deliberately.** A quiet sale protects who walks through the house and gives up reach. A full listing reaches everyone and gives up privacy. Both are legitimate. Choosing by default is not.
