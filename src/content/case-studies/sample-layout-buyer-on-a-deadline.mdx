## Why did they need to move on a deadline?

Open with the pressure the client was under and where it came from. A job start date, a lease ending, a sale that had already firmed up. Keep it to a short paragraph, in plain language and resist the urge to make it more dramatic than it was.

## What was the first thing they told me?

This is where the first person voice starts doing the work. What did the client say they wanted and how did that turn out to be different from what they actually needed? This is the section readers remember.

## Why that neighbourhood and not the obvious one?

Name the neighbourhoods that were considered and explain the reasoning. Link to the relevant pillar pages. This is the section that does the most for search, because it answers a comparison question a real person typed.

## What went wrong?

Every transaction has a moment. A failed inspection, a financing delay, a competing offer, an appraisal that came in short. Say what it was and do not sand the edges off it. A case study with no difficulty in it reads as marketing and gets ignored.

## What I did about it

The specific action, the reasoning behind it and what it cost or risked. Be concrete. Vague competence is not persuasive.

## The lesson

One paragraph that is useful to a reader who is not this client. This is what makes a case study worth reading for someone who will never be in the same situation.
