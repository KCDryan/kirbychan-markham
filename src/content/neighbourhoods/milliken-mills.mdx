## The short answer

Milliken Mills sits at the bottom of Markham, pressed against the Toronto border at Steeles Avenue. It has the best transit access in the city, the oldest volume housing stock outside Thornhill and, in Milliken Mills West, the lowest detached median of the neighbourhoods covered on this site from January to March 2026.

That combination makes it the most practical neighbourhood in the city and the least glamorous one. Both of those things are true at once.

## What homes cost

TRREB reports Milliken Mills as two communities. From January to March 2026 Milliken Mills East recorded 15 sales with a median of $1,095,000 and Milliken Mills West recorded 12 sales with a median of $1,080,000, both across all property types. The table above shows both.

The detached figures differ sharply between East and West and both samples are small, so do not read too much into one quarter. Within either part, price is mostly explained by two things: the age of the housing and how close a given street sits to Steeles, Kennedy or Warden. Interior streets trade meaningfully better than arterial frontage and that gap is where buyers who do their homework find value.

## Schools

Milliken Mills High School serves much of the area, alongside other YRDSB secondary options and YCDSB elementary schools. Confirm the catchment for the exact address, because boundaries are reviewed periodically.

## Parks, amenities and daily life

Community recreation, libraries and parks serve the area and retail along the Steeles and Kennedy corridors is extensive and genuinely diverse. For food shopping in particular this is one of the best served parts of the GTA.

The honest trade-off: traffic. Steeles and Kennedy are busy roads and they stay busy. If you buy on or near an arterial you will live with the noise and you will also find it harder to sell later.

## Transit and the commute

Milliken GO on the Stouffville line runs into Union Station. The station itself is in Scarborough, just south of Steeles. YRT routes serve the area and TTC connections at Steeles Avenue link into the Toronto network, which is a real advantage for anyone without a car.

Scheduled weekday morning trains take 35 minutes from Milliken GO to Union Station (GO Transit timetable, September 2026).

## Downsizing in Milliken Mills

Milliken Mills has an older mix of detached homes and semis alongside townhomes and condo apartments along the main corridors, which gives downsizers real choice between staying in a smaller freehold house or moving into a condo. The detached medians from January to March 2026, about $1,140,000 in Milliken Mills West and about $1,627,000 in Milliken Mills East, sit well above what a townhome or condo in the same corridors typically costs, so the gap is significant for anyone ready to trade space for lower upkeep.

The strongest argument for downsizing here rather than elsewhere in Markham is transit. Milliken GO, YRT routes and TTC connections at Steeles put a car-free lifestyle within reach in a way most of the city cannot match, which particularly suits downsizers who no longer want to drive everywhere. Interior streets away from Steeles and Kennedy are the quieter choice. Our [downsizing in Markham guide](/downsizing-markham/) covers the planning side of the move.

## Who Milliken Mills suits

It suits first time buyers who want a freehold house, people who commute into Toronto by transit, newcomers who want established amenities close by and investors looking at older stock near a station.

It does not suit buyers who want new construction, quiet streets throughout or the school reputation that drives north Markham pricing.
