## The short answer

Thornhill straddles Yonge Street and everything east of that line is Markham. It is the city's oldest established residential area, the closest to Toronto and the best served by transit.

The trade for that convenience is age. Much of the housing stock predates 1990 and the inspection reports look nothing like what you see in Wismer or Cornell.

## The Yonge Street split and why it matters

This is the single most common point of confusion for buyers. East of Yonge is the City of Markham. West of Yonge is the City of Vaughan. The two sides share a name, a postal prefix in places and very little else administratively.

Property tax rates differ. Permits go to different city halls. Some school catchments follow the line. Before you compare two Thornhill listings, confirm which municipality each one sits in.

Land value drives this market. On the larger mature lots you are frequently competing with builders and the house standing on the lot is close to irrelevant to what the property is worth. If you intend to live in the house rather than replace it, be clear eyed about that when you write an offer.

## Schools

Thornlea Secondary School is among the YRDSB options serving the area, alongside other secondary and elementary schools through both public boards. Catchments in an area split across two municipalities are more complicated than usual, so confirm the boundary for the exact address.

## Parks, amenities and daily life

The historic Thornhill village area, parks, ravine trails and long established retail along Yonge Street and Bayview Avenue shape daily life. This is the part of Markham that feels most like an extension of Toronto, because functionally it is.

The honest trade-off: older houses mean older wiring, older plumbing and older windows. Some pockets sit close to busy arterial roads. Both are manageable and both belong in your budget rather than in a surprise after closing.

## Transit and the commute

Viva bus rapid transit and YRT run along Yonge Street and Highway 7. TTC connections at Steeles Avenue put the Toronto subway network within reach, which no other part of Markham can say as easily. Highways 404 and 407 are both close.

## Who the Markham side of Thornhill suits

It suits buyers who want to be as close to Toronto as Markham allows, downsizers who have lived here for decades and buyers who want a mature lot and are prepared to renovate or rebuild.

It does not suit buyers who want a new build with a warranty or who need a large modern floor plan without doing the work themselves.
