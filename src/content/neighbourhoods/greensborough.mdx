## The short answer

Greensborough is the rare north Markham neighbourhood where the GO station is inside the community rather than a drive away. Mount Joy anchors it and a good share of the housing is genuinely within walking distance.

Combine that with heavy townhome supply and you get the most attainable entry point into north Markham for a buyer who wants a train commute.

The townhome supply is the story. It holds the entry price down and it means sellers face more direct competition than almost anywhere else in the city. If you are selling a townhome here, assume a buyer is looking at four near identical units the same weekend.

## Schools

Elementary schools serve the area through both public boards, with secondary options in north and east Markham. Boundaries are reviewed periodically, so confirm the catchment for the specific address.

## Parks, amenities and daily life

Parks, ponds and trail links run through the community and the streets are newer with wide sidewalks. Shopping is plaza based, along Markham Road and Bur Oak Avenue.

The honest trade-off: this is a young neighbourhood with a young tree canopy and little character housing. It is also on the northeast edge of the city, so driving anywhere west takes longer than the map suggests at rush hour.

## Transit and the commute

Mount Joy GO on the Stouffville line runs into Union Station and is the neighbourhood's main advantage. YRT routes connect south toward the Highway 7 corridor. Highway 407 access is east and south.

## Who Greensborough suits

It suits first time buyers, train commuters and investors looking at attached stock near transit.

It does not suit buyers who want a large lot, mature trees or a short drive to Highway 404.
