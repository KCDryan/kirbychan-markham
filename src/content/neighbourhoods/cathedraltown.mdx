## The short answer

Cathedraltown is the most visually distinctive neighbourhood in Markham. It was planned around the Cathedral of the Transfiguration and the architectural control that came with it produced streets that look designed rather than assembled.

You either respond to that or you do not. There is very little middle ground and that shows up in how houses sell here.

Two things to understand about pricing. First, the uniformity cuts both ways: comparables are easy, which keeps pricing honest and standing out as a seller is hard. Second, proximity to the cathedral grounds and to open space carries a premium that is not obvious from a listing photo.

## Architecture and the rules that come with it

The exterior consistency here is the product of design controls applied at build. Before you plan a change to a facade, a driveway or landscaping, read what is registered on title and check with the City. Buyers who assume a suburban free hand sometimes get an unwelcome surprise.

## Parks, amenities and daily life

The cathedral grounds and the surrounding open space are the neighbourhood's defining public realm. Parks and trail connections run through the community.

The honest trade-off: retail is thin inside the neighbourhood. Groceries, pharmacies and restaurants are a drive along Major Mackenzie Drive or Woodbine Avenue.

## Transit and the commute

YRT local routes run along the main roads. Highway 404 is a short drive west, which is the practical commuting route for most residents. There is no GO station inside the neighbourhood, so downtown commuters typically drive to Mount Joy or Unionville.

## Who Cathedraltown suits

It suits buyers who want newer housing with a strong sense of place, relocating families who value a coherent neighbourhood and anyone who responds to the architecture.

It does not suit buyers who want to renovate a facade freely, who need walkable retail or who want a short train commute.
