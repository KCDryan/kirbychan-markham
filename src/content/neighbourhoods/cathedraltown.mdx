## The short answer

Cathedraltown is the most visually distinctive neighbourhood in Markham. It was planned around the Cathedral of the Transfiguration and the architectural control that came with it produced streets that look designed rather than assembled.

You either respond to that or you do not. There is very little middle ground and that shows up in how houses sell here.

## What homes cost

From January to March 2026 TRREB recorded 12 sales in Cathedraltown with a median of $1,200,000 across all property types. The by type rows in the table above rest on only a few sales each, so read them with care.

Two things to understand about pricing. First, the uniformity cuts both ways: comparables are easy, which keeps pricing honest and standing out as a seller is hard. Second, proximity to the cathedral grounds and to open space carries a premium that is not obvious from a listing photo.

## Architecture and the rules that come with it

The exterior consistency here is the product of design controls applied at build. Before you plan a change to a facade, a driveway or landscaping, read what is registered on title and check with the City. Buyers who assume a suburban free hand sometimes get an unwelcome surprise.

## Parks, amenities and daily life

The cathedral grounds and the surrounding open space are the neighbourhood's defining public realm. Parks and trail connections run through the community.

The honest trade-off: retail is thin inside the neighbourhood. Groceries, pharmacies and restaurants are a drive along Major Mackenzie Drive or Woodbine Avenue.

## Transit and the commute

YRT local routes run along the main roads. Highway 404 is a short drive west, which is the practical commuting route for most residents. There is no GO station inside the neighbourhood, so downtown commuters typically drive to Mount Joy or Unionville.

Scheduled weekday morning trains take 57 minutes from Mount Joy GO and 41 minutes from Unionville GO to Union Station, plus the drive to the station (GO Transit timetable, September 2026).

## Thinking of selling in Cathedraltown?

Cathedraltown's detached and semi-detached homes, freehold townhomes and low rise condos all sit inside the same architectural controls, which makes comparables easier to pull than in most Markham neighbourhoods, yet still leaves lot position, condition and finishes to decide where a specific home lands. The median across all property types was $1,200,000 on 12 sales from January to March 2026, with detached homes closer to $1,810,000 (TRREB), though a sample this size moves quickly. A price range from recent comparable sales nearby remains the useful starting point before listing. ## Downsizing in Cathedraltown

A downsizer staying in Cathedraltown has a genuine choice between a freehold townhome and one of the low rise condos, alongside the smaller semis that make up part of the mix. From January to March 2026 the freehold townhouse median was about $1,190,000 against a detached median of about $1,810,000, a gap of roughly $620,000 for buyers trading a larger house for a smaller unit within the same community.

The architectural controls that give Cathedraltown its uniform streetscape are worth checking before you buy a smaller property here, since elevations, materials and colours were set at build and any exterior change should be confirmed against what is registered on title. Downsizers who value the cathedral grounds and open space over walkable retail tend to be the ones who settle in well. ## Who Cathedraltown suits

It suits buyers who want newer housing with a strong sense of place, relocating families who value a coherent neighbourhood and anyone who responds to the architecture.

It does not suit buyers who want to renovate a facade freely, who need walkable retail or who want a short train commute.
