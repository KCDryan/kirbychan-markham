## The short answer

There is no single month that names itself as the best time to sell a house in Markham. Activity tends to build through spring and early summer, but so do new listings, which means more competition for your home.

The Toronto Regional Real Estate Board (TRREB) publishes monthly [Market Watch](https://trreb.ca/market-data/market-watch/) figures, including for the City of Markham, if you want to see recent months. If you are asking about the best time to sell a house in Ontario more broadly, the same caution applies: past months describe what happened rather than what will happen. A better answer for you starts with your own plans, which we cover near the end.

## When do most homes sell in Markham?

Spring usually brings more buyers into the market and activity often stays strong into early summer. Late summer can be quieter and there is often another push in the fall. Activity typically slows in December and January, when fewer buyers are looking.

Read the seasonal pattern as a general shape across the year rather than a precise forecast. Month-to-month gaps can be small and they shift from one year to the next.

## When do sellers face the most competition from other listings?

New listings show how many other sellers came to market in the same month. They tend to rise in spring along with buyer activity, so a seller listing in spring often shares the market with more competing homes than in any other season. Fewer new listings typically appear in December.

Buyers and new listings do not always move together. A month with plenty of buyers can also be a month with plenty of choice, which affects how your home stands out.

If you want to understand how competing offers work when buyers do crowd in, read our guide to [multiple offers in Ontario](/blog/multiple-offers-ontario-how-they-work/).

## How long do homes take to sell in Markham and at what price?

Days on market and how close homes sell to asking price both change through the year and from one year to the next. TRREB's Market Watch reports these measures each month, so check the most recent figures rather than relying on a past season.

One caution about prices. Average and median prices move with the mix of homes that sold in a month. A change can come from a different mix of homes selling rather than from any one home being worth more or less. For that reason we do not treat monthly figures as proof that prices rise in spring. For the latest month in detail, see our [market reports](/market-reports/).

## How can you read TRREB Market Watch yourself?

Each monthly Market Watch report includes a table for the City of Markham. A few habits make it easier to read:

- **Look at new listings next to sales.** Together they give a rough sense of how much choice buyers had that month.
- **Compare the same month across years.** Comparing one month with the next mostly shows the seasons at work.
- **Treat averages with care.** A month where more large homes sold will show a higher average without any single home changing in value.
- **Read several months together.** One report is a snapshot. A run of months shows whether conditions are steady or shifting.

## Which timing factors depend on you as a seller?

The seasonal pattern cannot see your situation. These are the factors that sit with the seller:

- **Your next home.** If you are buying as well as selling, your purchase dates matter more than the month. Our post on [selling first or buying first](/blog/sell-first-or-buy-first-downsizing-markham/) explains both paths.
- **Your closing date flexibility.** A sale that needs to close on a fixed day has fewer options than one with room to move.
- **Your school year.** Some families prefer to move between school years. School boundaries are set by the school board and must be confirmed for the exact address you are moving to.
- **How long preparation takes.** Repairs and cleaning take time. Our [selling timeline](/blog/selling-a-house-in-markham-timeline/) lays out the steps from preparation to closing.
- **Your reason for moving.** Sellers who are [upsizing](/upsizing-markham/) or [downsizing](/downsizing-markham/) often have a date they are working toward.

Talk to your lawyer, accountant and mortgage professional about how timing affects your own finances.

## How should you prepare your home before a spring listing?

If you are aiming for spring, the work starts in winter. Booking trades, clearing out storage and gathering paperwork can each take longer than planned, so leave room in your schedule.

- **Fix the small things first.** A dripping tap, a loose railing or worn caulking is easier to repair before photos than to negotiate after an offer.
- **Gather your documents early.** Permits for past renovations, appliance warranties and recent utility bills help you answer buyer questions quickly.
- **Plan for the outside.** Gardens can still look bare early in the season, so keep the walkway, porch and front door clean and tidy.
- **Ask about photography.** Your agent can suggest when each room looks its best.

## How do the school year and your buyer's plans affect timing?

Your buyer has a calendar too. Some families prefer to close over the summer so children can start the year in a new school. A buyer who is selling a home of their own may need a closing that lines up with that sale. Knowing which dates matter to the other side can help you agree on terms that also suit your own move.

## What should you ask an agent about timing?

- How much competition have homes like mine faced in recent months?
- How long should we allow for preparation before listing?
- Would a pre-listing inspection help in my case?
- How would you approach pricing if we list in a quieter month?
- Which closing dates make sense given where I am moving next?
