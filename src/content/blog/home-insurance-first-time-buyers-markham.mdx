## The short answer

Home insurance for first-time buyers in Markham is one of the last items on the closing list and one of the easiest to get wrong. A typical policy protects the building and your belongings and covers you if someone is hurt on your property. What it often does not cover, unless you add it, is water that comes up through a floor drain or in from outside.

That gap matters more in some Markham neighbourhoods than others. The City of Markham has spent more than a decade on flood control in older parts of Thornhill, Unionville and Markham Village. It publishes where and why. This post explains how home insurance works for a first home in Markham, what to ask about and what the City's own pages say. It is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Your insurer, broker and real estate lawyer can speak to your situation.

## What home insurance covers on a Markham home

The [Financial Consumer Agency of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/insurance/home.html) (FCAC) describes home insurance as protection for your home and belongings in case of theft, loss or damage. It may also help with additional living expenses, such as a hotel, if you cannot live in the home for a while after a covered loss.

FCAC says most buyers need two types of coverage:

- **Personal property coverage** for loss or damage to your home and your possessions.
- **Liability coverage** for legal liability if you injure someone or damage their property, for example a visitor who falls on your front steps.

Insurers call the unexpected events a policy covers "perils". FCAC lists fire, wind-related weather, ice and hail, theft, vandalism and water damage from inside your home, such as a broken pipe. It also lists what policies may not cover: earthquakes, landslides, floods and sewer backup.

Two settlement terms are worth knowing before you compare quotes:

| Term | What FCAC says it means |
| --- | --- |
| Actual cash value | The cost of the item when it was new, minus depreciation for age and condition |
| Replacement value | The actual cost to replace the item after a covered loss |
| Deductible | The part of a claim you pay before the insurer pays the rest. You always pay it when you claim |
| Loss payee clause | Names your mortgage lender as beneficiary, so a payout for damage to the home can go to the lender up to the mortgage balance |

## Sewer backup and overland flooding: the gaps to ask about

The Government of Canada's [overland flood insurance page](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/policing/emergencies/preparedness/get-prepared/hazards-emergencies/floods/flood-risk/overland-flood-insurance.html) is plain about this. Water damage such as a burst pipe or a leaking hot water tank is a basic coverage on home policies. Coverage for flooding is not typically included in a standard policy.

Insurers define each type of water damage differently, so there is no single "flood insurance". The page lists the add-ons, called endorsements, to ask about:

- **Sewer backup**, when water or sewage enters through floor drains, toilets, sinks or sump drainage connected to the sewer.
- **Overland flooding**, from heavy rain, snowmelt or overflowing rivers and lakes entering the home.
- **Sump pump failure**, which some insurers treat as part of overland flood coverage.

Availability depends on the flood risk your insurer assigns to the address. FCAC adds that in a very high risk area an insurer may not offer flood coverage at all. If it does, it may charge a higher premium. The flood deductible is usually higher than for other types of damage.

The stakes are real. The same federal page says floods are the most common and costly natural hazard in Canada and account for about half of all home insurance claims. It puts the average cost to repair a flooded basement at over $40,000.

Do not count on government help instead. Ontario's [Disaster Recovery Assistance for Ontarians](https://www.ontario.ca/page/apply-disaster-recovery-assistance) programme says it is not intended to replace insurance and that insurance payments are deducted from eligible costs. For sewer backup, it says only low-income households can receive help, because insurance is widely available for that damage.

## Why basement flooding matters in older Markham neighbourhoods

The City of Markham's [Flood Control Program](https://www.markham.ca/neighbourhood-services/water-sewer/water-and-sewer-programs) is a 30-year plan that Council approved in February 2013 to improve storm drainage and limit surface and basement flooding in urban areas.

Three points from the City's page are useful for a buyer:

1. **Age of the area matters.** The City says it prioritized drainage systems built before 1978, when modern stormwater standards were implemented. It says areas built before 1978 are more susceptible to flooding because they were not designed for very large storms.
2. **The City names its priority areas.** Upgrades were prioritized in West Thornhill, the Don Mills Channel (Woodbine and Denison area) and Markham Village and Unionville, based on the known locations of basement flooding clusters.
3. **Every owner helps pay for it.** Apart from federal and provincial grants and gas tax funding, the City says the program is paid for by a stormwater fee charged to all residential and non-residential property owners on the final tax bill.

A second City program targeted the same neighbourhoods. The City's [basement flooding and sewer back-up page](https://www.markham.ca/neighbourhood-services/water-sewer/basement-flooding-and-sewer-back-prevention) says its downspout disconnection program, completed as of December 6, 2022, found and disconnected downspouts draining into the sanitary sewer within the high risk of basement flooding area in Thornhill, Unionville and Markham Village. Those connections push rainwater into the sanitary sewer in heavy storms and can cause sewer backups.

None of this means a particular house has flooded or will. It means that if you are buying in [Markham-side Thornhill](/thornhill-markham/), [Unionville](/unionville-markham/) or [Markham Village](/markham-village-markham/), sewer backup coverage is a question to settle early, ideally before your conditions expire. Ask the seller about past water in the basement and ask your home inspector to look at the grading, downspouts and any backwater valve. Our guide to [what to check on older Markham homes](/blog/older-homes-markham-what-to-check/) covers the inspection side.

The City's Flood Control Program also includes subsidies for residents to improve their private plumbing, through its Private Plumbing Protection Program. Terms change, so confirm what is currently offered with the City at 905.477.5530 or customerservice@markham.ca before you budget for it.

## What affects your home insurance premium in Markham

FCAC lists the factors insurers may consider when they price a policy:

- the type of home, such as a single family home, semi-detached or condo
- the home's materials, age, size, location and replacement value
- the value of the property and contents being insured
- the distance to a fire hydrant or fire station
- your claims history
- the policy, coverage and deductible you choose

FCAC also notes that in some provinces insurers may charge more based on your credit score. It says combining home and car insurance can sometimes earn a discount. Premiums vary between insurers, so FCAC's advice is to shop around and compare quotes.

On its [flood proofing page for businesses](https://www.markham.ca/neighbourhood-services/water-sewer/flood-proofing-program) near the Don Mills Channel, the City says it is challenging the insurance industry to consider Markham's flood control investments in rate setting. It encourages owners to share information about that work with their insurance agents at renewal and to make sure their coverage includes sewer backup.

## Condo or freehold: which policy a Markham buyer needs

The kind of home you buy changes the kind of policy you need.

| You are buying | What your own policy covers | What else to review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detached, semi or freehold townhouse | The building, your belongings and your liability | Sewer backup and overland flood add-ons for the address |
| Condo apartment or condo townhouse | Condo insurance: the inside of your unit, your locker, belongings, improvements and personal liability | The condo corporation's policy, which covers the outside structure and common areas |

That split comes from FCAC's description of condo insurance. FCAC also says condo insurance may pay for improvements you or a previous owner made to the unit and for damage your unit causes to other units or common areas. For a condo, the corporation's insurance details are part of the documents you review with the [status certificate when you buy a Markham condo](/blog/condo-status-certificate-markham/).

Home insurance is also separate from title insurance, which protects ownership rather than the building. Our post on [title insurance in Ontario](/blog/title-insurance-ontario/) explains the difference.

## Frozen pipes, basement suites and other things insurers ask about

A few details catch first-time owners out in their first Markham winter.

- **Frozen pipes while you travel.** FCAC says insurers do not cover events they consider predictable and related to maintenance. Its example: if you are away for more than 4 days in a row in cold weather, a policy may not cover damage from frozen pipes. Read your policy's wording on absences. The City's [Homeowner Maintenance Guide](https://www.markham.ca/neighbourhood-services/water-sewer/homeowner-maintenance-guide) suggests leaving the heat at a minimum of 15 C, shutting off the main indoor water supply and draining taps and asking someone to check the house.
- **The pipe from the street is yours past the property line.** The City says it maintains the pipes from the water main to the municipal property line and the owner is responsible from the property line into the home. Ask your insurer how a break in that service line would be treated.
- **Basement apartments and rented rooms.** FCAC says you must tell your insurer if you share your home or rent part of it. If the home you are buying has a second unit, our post on [a legal basement suite versus a finished basement](/blog/legal-basement-suite-vs-finished-basement-key-differences/) explains what to confirm with the City.
- **Working from home.** FCAC says home insurance is not business insurance and using your home for purposes your insurer does not know about may cancel your policy.
- **Full disclosure on the application.** [FSRA](https://www.fsrao.ca/consumers/property-and-other-insurance), the Ontario insurance regulator, lists your responsibilities: disclose previous claims, renovations and home-based business activities. FSRA also warns that if you undervalue your home or contents when you buy the policy, you might not have enough coverage.

## Your home insurance checklist before closing in Markham

Work through this once your offer is firm, alongside the rest of the [closing costs for a Markham home](/blog/closing-costs-buying-home-markham/).

1. Ask your lender and real estate lawyer what proof of insurance they need, in whose names and by what date.
2. Get at least two quotes for the exact address, naming the property type, age and any basement unit.
3. Ask each insurer, in writing, whether the quote includes sewer backup, overland flood and sump pump failure coverage and what the limits and deductibles are for each.
4. Ask whether the policy pays replacement value or actual cash value for the building and your belongings.
5. For a condo, compare your unit policy with the corporation's coverage in the status certificate package.
6. Ask your broker or insurer whether your belongings are covered while they are being moved. CMHC suggests making this call before moving day.
7. Check that the insurer or agent is licensed through FSRA or that your broker is registered with the Registered Insurance Brokers of Ontario.
8. Diarize the policy start date so it lines up with your closing date. Our guide to [closing day in Ontario](/blog/closing-day-ontario-markham/) walks through the rest of that day.
