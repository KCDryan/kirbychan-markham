## The short answer

Overnight parking in Markham is simple to describe and easy to get wrong after a move. The City does not allow parking on its streets between 2:30 a.m. and 6:00 a.m., all year, unless signs permit it or you hold a valid exemption or monthly permit. For a first-time buyer, that one rule decides how many cars a home can really hold.

A listing may say "parking for 3". On many Markham townhouses and semis, that means a single garage and a driveway and nothing on the street overnight. If your household has more cars than the property has spaces, the gap is a cost you will feel every week.

This post explains the City's rules as its parking pages state them today, November 23, 2026, including a change that starts today. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Markham's overnight street parking rule

The City's parking pages repeat the same line: parking is prohibited on Markham streets between 2:30 a.m. and 6:00 a.m. unless signs permit it. The winter parking page adds that this applies year-round without a valid exemption.

There are two ways to park on the street during those hours:

- an **overnight exemption**, which covers a single night for a specific plate
- a **monthly overnight parking permit**, which covers a calendar month, but only in approved zones

Neither one works during snow clearing. More on that below.

| Option | Who can get it | What it costs today | Main limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Overnight exemption | Residents and visitors, applied for online | No charge during the fee pause that started November 23, 2026 | Up to 10 per licence plate a year; not valid during winter maintenance; no more than 48 continuous hours in one spot |
| Monthly overnight permit | Markham residents in approved zones | Bought through the City's permit portal (non-refundable) | Your own street only; no more than 24 continuous hours in one spot; no guaranteed space; not valid during winter maintenance |

## Overnight parking exemptions in Markham: what changed on November 23, 2026

The City says that starting November 23, 2026 the $6 fee for overnight on-street parking exemptions is paused. It says it heard feedback from the community, stakeholder groups and members of Council and is using the pause to review the program. The pause stays in place until a new policy is adopted.

During the pause, residents and visitors can apply online at no charge. The maximum is 10 exemptions per licence plate a year. The City's own example: if you have already used 8 this year, you have 2 left. It also says that beginning in 2027 each plate will be eligible for up to 10 exemptions per calendar year at no charge.

The City describes exemptions as meant for driveway repairs and out of town overnight guests. Its other conditions are worth knowing before you count on one:

- You still need an exemption during the fee pause. Only the fee is paused.
- Exemptions are not granted to vehicles with unpaid parking tickets.
- A vehicle on a daily exemption cannot stay in the same spot for more than 48 continuous hours.
- Commercial motor vehicles are refused even if the system accepts them. The City's list includes trailers, recreational vehicles, cube vans, boats and snowmobiles.
- Exemptions are only for plated vehicles and the City does not approve on-street parking on private property.
- Dumpsters and container bins on the road need a road occupancy permit, not an exemption.

The City also warns that inaccurate information on the form can lead to a penalty and that you get an email confirmation when an exemption is entered correctly.

Ten nights a year is enough for visiting family or a driveway resealing. It is not a plan for a second or third car that has nowhere else to go.

## Monthly overnight parking permits and approved zones

A monthly overnight parking permit is the City's option for regular street parking at night. The City says parking permits are only issued to residents of Markham in approved zones. That is the key question for a buyer: is the street you are looking at in one?

The City's guidelines for permit holders say:

- A new permit needs proof of residency (for example a driver's licence or utility bill) and proof of vehicle ownership (the vehicle registration).
- Permits are bought for a specific calendar month and expire on the last day of that month. You can buy one month in advance, for up to 3 months.
- A permit is valid only for the licence plate it was bought for.
- You must park on your own street and only where overnight permit parking is authorized under the City's Parking By-law 2005-188.
- A vehicle cannot stay in the same spot for more than 24 continuous hours.
- Having a permit does not guarantee a space will be available.
- Permits are non-refundable and the City can revoke them.
- Commercial motor vehicles are not eligible.

The City also notes that you may need to move a vehicle for tree pruning, paving or curb work.

The practical step for a buyer is to ask before the offer is firm. Call Parking Operations at 905.477.7000 or email parkingadmin@markham.ca with the street name and ask whether it is in an approved monthly permit zone. A seller who parks on the street every night may or may not hold a permit. Either way, the City says a permit is valid only for the licence plate it was bought for.

## Winter parking in Markham: when every permit stops working

Winter is when the street parking question becomes serious. The City says parking on all City streets is strictly prohibited during winter maintenance and snow clearing, on both sides of the street. Winter maintenance includes snow clearing, salting and sanding.

During those operations, all existing parking permits and overnight exemptions are suspended. Vehicles that interfere can be ticketed and towed. The City lists a $150 penalty for each of these:

| Violation during winter maintenance | Penalty |
| --- | --- |
| Parked so as to interfere with snow removal | $150 |
| Parked so as to interfere with winter maintenance of the highway | $150 |
| Parked so as to interfere with the clearing of snow from a sidewalk | $150 |
| Parked so as to interfere with winter maintenance of a sidewalk | $150 |

The City says snow clearing may begin as soon as snowfall starts and may continue for up to 24 hours after it ends. Its Winter Maintenance App shows when crews are working and when operations are complete. Street parking may resume only after that.

So even a household with a valid monthly permit needs somewhere off the street to put every car on snow days. ## Driveways and curb cuts: what you cannot change yourself

Many first-time buyers assume a driveway can be widened later to fit another car. On the City side, the curb is not yours to change.

The City says you need a permit to make changes to a curb near your property and that you may not modify a curb on your own. A curb cut is the lowered part of the curb that lets cars cross into a driveway. Widening one or creating a new one needs a permit. The City charges a fee that covers a site inspection and an assessment of which changes you can make and it points to By-law 158-93 for the size of changes allowed.

That means extra parking is a question to research before you buy, not a renovation to count on after closing. Ask the City by email at customerservice@markham.ca with "Curb Modification" in the subject line. Changes on the lot itself can raise separate zoning questions, so our post on the [minor variance process in Markham](/blog/minor-variance-markham-deck-addition/) is useful background and your lawyer can confirm what applies to the specific property.

## Parking questions to ask before buying a home in Markham

The same rules apply City-wide, whether you are looking at a townhouse in [Cornell](/cornell-markham/), a semi in [Milliken Mills](/milliken-mills-markham/) or a condo townhouse near downtown Markham. Use this list on every showing:

1. Count the real spaces: garage (is it usable for a car or full of storage?) and driveway length and width. Do this in person.
2. Count your household's cars today and in five years.
3. Ask whether the street is in an approved monthly overnight permit zone. Parking Operations can answer for a specific street.
4. Check the street for signs. Signs can permit or restrict parking beyond the general rule.
5. For a condo townhouse, read what the status certificate and declaration say about parking spaces and visitor parking. Our post on [condo status certificates in Markham](/blog/condo-status-certificate-markham/) explains what to look for.
6. Ask your lawyer before the offer is firm if a planned driveway change, curb cut or extra space matters to your decision.
7. Plan for snow days. Every car needs an off-street space when winter maintenance is under way.

Parking also affects your budget. If the answer to question 3 is no, the cost may be a second home on your list rather than a second car on the street. Our [first-time home buyer guide to Markham](/first-time-home-buyers-markham/) covers the rest of the purchase, from budget to closing.

## What to do next

Before your next showing, write down how many cars your household needs to park every night, then check each home against that number and the City's rules above. If a street matters to your plan, confirm its permit status with the City in writing.
