## The short answer

* A reverse mortgage lets you tap home equity while staying in your Markham house, but the loan balance grows with interest every year it's outstanding.
* Selling converts all of your equity into cash immediately, with no ongoing debt, but you'll need somewhere else to live.
* Many long-time owners have significant equity either way, so the real question is lifestyle, not just money.
* Reverse mortgages come with setup costs, legal advice requirements and long-term interest, so run the numbers before committing.
* A local agent can give you a realistic sense of what your home would sell for today through a [free home valuation](/home-valuation/), which makes comparing the two options much easier.
* This post is general information. Confirm your own situation with a mortgage professional and a lawyer.

## Why This Decision Comes Up for Markham Homeowners

Markham has a lot of long-time homeowners sitting on decades of appreciation. If you bought in the 1990s or early 2000s, your home may be worth well above what you paid and a big share of your net worth is tied up in that equity. At some point, many owners start asking how to access that value without giving up the home they've lived in for years.

This usually comes up around retirement, after a health change or when a spouse passes away and one income has to stretch further. It also comes up when family members need help, whether that's a down payment for a child or covering care costs for yourself. Whatever the trigger, the reverse mortgage or sell question is really about turning a paper asset into something usable.

## How a Reverse Mortgage Actually Works

A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against your home that doesn't require regular payments. Instead, the interest is added to the balance owing. According to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, you typically repay when you sell, move out of your home, the last borrower dies or you default on the loan.

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says reverse mortgages are usually for homeowners aged 55 or older. Lenders also consider the home's condition, type and value. Every lender sets its own limits, so whether your Markham home qualifies is something to confirm with the lender.

The amount you can borrow is a percentage of your home's current value. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says you may usually borrow up to 55 percent, with the maximum depending on your age, the home and the lender. Ask any lender you're considering for a specific quote once you have a current appraisal.

### What It Costs

Reverse mortgages aren't free money. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada lists appraisal fees, set-up fees, prepayment penalties, legal fees and closing costs. It also notes that independent legal advice is required in some provinces and recommended everywhere. Interest rates on reverse mortgages are usually higher than on a standard mortgage or home equity line of credit and the interest is added to what you owe, so the balance grows over time.

Over a long period, that growing balance can reduce the equity you keep. It can suit some situations, but it's worth asking the lender for a projection of the balance over time before deciding, not just the amount you'd receive upfront.

## What Selling Gives You That a Reverse Mortgage Doesn't

Selling your Markham home converts your equity into cash, after selling costs and without any ongoing debt attached. There's no interest accruing against the property, no annual balance to track and no complications for whoever inherits your estate.

Many owners who sell use the proceeds to buy something smaller, in Markham or elsewhere and pocket the difference. Others move into a rental or a retirement community and invest the proceeds for income. Either way, you have full control over the money and how it's used, which is different from a reverse mortgage where the lender's terms shape what happens to the equity over time.

The tradeoff, obviously, is that you have to move. For some homeowners that's the whole objection and it's a legitimate one. Moving after decades in the same house is a real transition, not just a financial decision.

## Questions to Ask Before You Decide

Before choosing between a reverse mortgage and selling, it helps to get specific answers rather than general impressions. A few worth asking:

* What would my Markham home actually sell for in today's market, after typical selling costs?
* How much would a reverse mortgage lender offer me and what would the balance look like in 10 or 15 years with interest?
* Do I want to stay in this specific home or am I open to something smaller or different?
* How important is preserving equity for my family or estate?
* What are my monthly costs likely to be if I stay versus if I move?

Getting a real number for what your home would sell for is often the missing piece. Many owners overestimate or underestimate their equity, which skews the whole comparison. A current market evaluation from a local agent gives you a useful baseline to compare against any reverse mortgage offer.
