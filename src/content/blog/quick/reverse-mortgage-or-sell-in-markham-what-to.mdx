## The short answer

* A reverse mortgage lets you tap home equity while staying in your Markham house, but the loan balance grows with interest every year it's outstanding.
* Selling converts all of your equity into cash immediately, with no ongoing debt, but you'll need somewhere else to live.
* Markham's strong home values mean many owners have significant equity either way, so the real question is lifestyle, not just money.
* Reverse mortgages come with setup costs, legal advice requirements and long-term interest, so run the numbers before committing.
* A local agent can give you a realistic sense of what your home would sell for today, which makes comparing the two options much easier.

## Why This Decision Comes Up for Markham Homeowners

Markham has a lot of long-time homeowners sitting on decades of appreciation. If you bought in the 1990s or early 2000s, your home is likely worth several times what you paid and a big share of your net worth is tied up in that equity. At some point, many owners start asking how to access that value without giving up the home they've lived in for years.

This usually comes up around retirement, after a health change or when a spouse passes away and one income has to stretch further. It also comes up when family members need help, whether that's a down payment for a child or covering care costs for yourself. Whatever the trigger, the reverse mortgage or sell question is really about turning a paper asset into something usable.

## How a Reverse Mortgage Actually Works

A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against your home that doesn't require regular payments. Instead, the interest accrues and gets added to the balance owing. You (or your estate) repay the loan when you sell, move out permanently or pass away.

To qualify, you typically need to be 55 or older and the home has to meet the lender's criteria for location, condition and value. Most Markham properties, from detached homes in Unionville to condos near the GO station, tend to qualify without issue given the area's home values, but every lender sets its own limits.

The amount you can borrow is usually a percentage of your appraised home value and that percentage rises with your age. Younger applicants in their late 50s typically qualify for a smaller share than someone in their 80s. Ask any lender you're considering for a specific quote once you have a current appraisal, since the exact percentage and rates change over time.

### What It Costs

Reverse mortgages aren't free money. You'll pay an appraisal fee, a legal fee (independent legal advice is mandatory in Canada before you sign) and often a lender setup fee. Interest rates on reverse mortgages also tend to run higher than a standard mortgage or home equity line of credit and that interest compounds over the life of the loan since you're not paying it down.

Over ten or fifteen years, that compounding can eat into a meaningful chunk of your equity. It's not a bad product for the right situation, but it's worth seeing a full amortization projection from the lender before deciding, not just the amount you'd receive upfront.

## What Selling Gives You That a Reverse Mortgage Doesn't

Selling your Markham home converts all of your equity into cash, immediately and without any ongoing debt attached. There's no interest accruing against the property, no annual balance to track and no complications for whoever inherits your estate.

Many owners who sell use the proceeds to buy something smaller, in Markham or elsewhere and pocket the difference. Others move into a rental or a retirement community and invest the proceeds for income. Either way, you have full control over the money and how it's used, which is different from a reverse mortgage where the lender's terms shape what happens to the equity over time.

The tradeoff, obviously, is that you have to move. For some homeowners that's the whole objection and it's a legitimate one. Moving after decades in the same house is a real transition, not just a financial decision.

## Questions to Ask Before You Decide

Before choosing between a reverse mortgage and selling, it helps to get specific answers rather than general impressions. A few worth asking:

* What would my Markham home actually sell for in today's market, after typical selling costs?
* How much would a reverse mortgage lender offer me and what would the balance look like in 10 or 15 years with interest?
* Do I want to stay in this specific home or am I open to something smaller or different?
* How important is preserving equity for my family or estate?
* What are my monthly costs likely to be if I stay versus if I move?

Getting a real number for what your home would sell for is often the missing piece. Many owners overestimate or underestimate their equity, which skews the whole comparison. A current market evaluation from a local agent gives you a solid baseline to compare against any reverse mortgage offer.

## How Markham's Market Affects This Decision

Markham's housing market has held its value well over the years, particularly in established neighbourhoods like Unionville, Cornell and Berczy Village. That works in your favour either way: strong equity supports a bigger reverse mortgage offer and it also supports a strong sale price if you go that route.

One thing worth factoring in: if you're thinking about downsizing in Markham rather than leaving the area entirely, smaller homes, condos and townhomes locally have their own price trends that are worth understanding before you assume selling nets you a certain amount left over. Sometimes the gap between a large detached home and a smaller local property is less than people expect, which changes the math on selling versus staying with a reverse mortgage.
