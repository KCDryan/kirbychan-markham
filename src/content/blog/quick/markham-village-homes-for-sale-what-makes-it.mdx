## Where is Markham Village and why does its layout look different

Markham Village sits along Main Street Markham, roughly between Highway 7 and 16th Avenue, east of Highway 404. It is one of the oldest settled parts of the city, with roots going back to the early 19th century, long before most of the subdivisions that define Markham's reputation today.

That age shows up in the streets themselves. Lots were laid out over decades by individual landowners rather than a single developer, so you will find wide frontages next to narrow ones, deep backyards next to shallow ones and homes set at different distances from the road. Compare that to a community built in a five or ten year window, where every lot on a street was drawn from the same plan and the results look it.

### A heritage district with real history

The Markham Village Heritage Conservation District was created in 1991 and is one of the city's oldest and largest heritage districts, covering around 700 properties. It is made up of three sub-areas: Vinegar Hill, centred on the Rouge River where early mills once stood; Markham Village itself, with its commercial core and residential streets near the railway station; and Mount Joy, the youngest of the three, concentrated along Main Street and Peter Street.

Architectural styles across these pockets include Ontario Vernacular, Queen Anne, Georgian, Regency and Italianate details, several dating to the mid and late 1800s. The oldest surviving structure in Markham, the Philip Eckardt Log House, dates to 1800 and while it is not in this specific district, it shows just how far back the area's building history goes.

## Old Markham Village homes for sale versus newer Markham communities

Most of Markham's housing stock was built from the 1990s onward, during a period of fast, planned growth. Communities like Berczy Village, Cathedral Town and the newer pockets around Swan Lake Village were designed with consistent lot sizes, repeated house models and curved subdivision streets meant to maximize density and parking efficiency.

Old Markham Village real estate is the opposite story. Streets follow the original village grid, lots vary in size and shape from one property to the next and houses were built one at a time over roughly two centuries rather than in a single construction cycle. If you walk Main Street Markham, you can often date a building within a few decades just by looking at its roofline and trim.

This matters for buyers in a practical way. A newer community gives you predictability: you generally know what to expect from one listing to the next because the builder repeated a handful of floor plans. Markham Village gives you character and individuality, but it also means every property needs its own careful look, since no two homes here were built to the same standard or era.

### What this means for townhouse buyers

If you are specifically looking at a Markham town house for sale, know that most newer townhouse developments sit outside the historic core, in communities built after 2000. Within Markham Village proper, you are more likely to find detached century homes and smaller infill houses than large townhouse blocks, though some newer infill and conversions do appear at the edges of the district.

## Buying a heritage-designated property: what actually changes

Owning a home inside the Markham Village Heritage Conservation District does not stop you from living in it normally, renovating the interior or updating systems like heating and plumbing. What it does affect is the exterior.

Changes that are visible from the street, things like windows, siding, rooflines, porches and additions, typically require a heritage permit before work can start. The City reviews these applications against the conservation district plan, which exists to protect the area's "village-like character" as new growth happens around it. The City has been working through an update to this plan, since the original version dates back to 1991 and a lot has changed in the decades since. Anyone buying with renovation plans should confirm the current permit process and timeline directly with the City's heritage planning staff before finalizing an offer.

This is also worth raising with your lawyer during the purchase. A heritage designation or inclusion in the Markham Register of Property of Cultural Heritage Value or Interest, should show up in title searches and disclosure, but it is worth asking the question directly rather than assuming.

## What to look for when comparing listings

Because the area is so mixed, two listings on the same street in Markham Village can represent very different buying decisions. Before you get attached to a photo, check a few things.

* Confirm whether the specific property is inside the designated heritage conservation district boundary, since not every nearby street is included.
* Ask when the home was actually built and whether any major systems, like the roof, wiring or foundation, have been updated since.
* Check lot dimensions directly rather than assuming, since frontages and depths vary more here than in a standard subdivision.
* If renovation or an addition is part of your plan, ask about the heritage permit process before you remove your financing condition.
* Compare the walk to Main Street Markham and nearby amenities, since proximity varies more across this area than in a planned community with one central layout.
