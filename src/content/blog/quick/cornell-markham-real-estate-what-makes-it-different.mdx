## The short answer

* Cornell was designed in the early 1990s by Duany Plater-Zyberk and Associates after winning a provincial design competition, making it Canada's landmark new urbanist community.
* Homes have rear laneway garages and coach houses above them, front porches close to the sidewalk and a grid of narrower streets instead of curving cul-de-sacs.
* The neighbourhood sits in northeast Markham, roughly bounded by Highway 407, 16th Avenue, Ninth Line and Donald Cousens Parkway.
* Housing stock is a genuine mix: detached homes, townhouses and laneway coach houses, which is unusual for a single Markham neighbourhood.
* Resale activity in Cornell moves with the broader York Region market, so timing and price depend on the current mix of listings and buyer demand.

## Why Cornell Markham Real Estate Looks Different From the Rest of Markham

Drive through most of Markham and you see the familiar suburban pattern: wide curving streets, double garages facing the road and houses set well back behind long driveways. Cornell breaks from that on purpose. It was master planned starting in the 1990s as a complete, multi-phase community rather than a series of unrelated subdivisions and the plan drew directly from new urbanist design thinking.

The story behind it is one of the more interesting pieces of Ontario planning history. The province ended up owning roughly 500 acres of land in this part of Markham that had originally been expropriated for a planned Pickering airport that was never built. Instead of releasing the land for conventional development, Ontario ran an international design competition in 1992. Duany Plater-Zyberk and Associates, the Miami firm closely associated with new urbanism, won that competition and led a five day public design session that drew more than a thousand residents to its final presentation.

## New Urbanist Community Markham: What the Principles Actually Mean

New urbanism is a planning philosophy built around a few core ideas: walkable streets, a connected grid rather than dead-end loops, a genuine mix of housing types and buildings placed close to the street so the public realm feels active rather than empty. Cornell is one of the clearest Canadian examples of this approach put into practice at scale and it remains a reference point for planners studying new urbanist community design in Markham and beyond.

Academic research on Cornell has found a mixed but largely positive result. Walkable streets and a genuine mix of housing types, including more affordable options, were successfully carried through from plan to finished neighbourhood. Mixed-use development, meaning shops and services woven directly into residential blocks, was harder to achieve and is less present than the original vision called for. That gap between the plan and the built reality is worth knowing if walkability near daily errands matters to you, since most commercial amenity sits at the neighbourhood's edges rather than scattered through it.

## Cornell Markham Houses With Laneway Garages: Why They Were Built This Way

The rear laneway is probably the single most recognizable feature of Cornell Markham houses. Instead of a garage door facing the street, cars park off a narrow lane behind each block, often with a coach house or apartment built above the garage. These were among the first laneway homes built in Ontario and they let the street-facing side of each house keep a front porch, a short walkway and a more pedestrian-scaled frontage.

The reasoning was straightforward: a street lined with garage doors discourages people from sitting outside or walking past, while a street lined with porches does the opposite. Whether that plays out the way planners hoped is debated. Some observers note that despite the design, many residents still drive for most trips and the streets can feel quieter than the plan intended. Others point out that the laneway homes themselves have become a useful, lower-cost entry point into the neighbourhood, functioning as rental units or starter homes for buyers who want to be in Cornell without the price tag of a full detached house.

## Walkable Neighbourhoods in Markham: How Cornell Compares

Markham has several newer communities that borrow a few walkable design ideas, like sidewalks on both sides of the street or a park within a short walk of most homes. Cornell goes further than most of them because the walkability was the organizing principle of the whole plan, not an add-on. Every street has a sidewalk, blocks are shorter than typical suburban blocks and the grid pattern means there are usually several route options to get from one point to another instead of a single collector road funnelling all traffic.

That said, buyers comparing walkable neighbourhoods in Markham should look at the street pattern itself before assuming walkability. Some parts of Cornell have a fairly maze-like layout within the broader grid, which can make walking routes less direct than they first appear on a map. It is worth walking the actual route from a prospective home to the school, park or transit stop you care about rather than relying on straight-line distance.

## Cornell Markham Community Living: Parks, Schools and Amenities

Cornell was planned with civic buildings and green space distributed through the neighbourhood rather than concentrated at one edge, which is part of why it reads as a complete community rather than just a housing development. The area includes several parks, schools within walking distance for many homes and Markham Stouffville Hospital sits adjacent to the community, which is a practical plus for families and a factor some buyers specifically look for.

A mid-rise apartment building and a seniors' residence sit near the centre of the community, adding a bit of the density and age mix that new urbanist planning calls for. Population in the broader Cornell area has been reported at around twelve thousand residents across its sub-areas, reflecting a fairly dense, built-out neighbourhood rather than a sparse, half-finished one. As with any figure like this, check current City of Markham or census data if a precise population number matters to your decision.

## Cornell Markham Homes for Sale: What the Market Looks Like Now

Homes for sale in Cornell Markham typically span detached houses on narrower-than-average lots, townhouses and coach houses above laneway garages. That mix means price points vary more within Cornell than they do in a lot of single-style Markham subdivisions, which can work in a buyer's favour if you are flexible on housing type but firm on location.

Average sale prices in Cornell move with broader shifts in the York Region market and with the specific mix of homes selling in a given period. A run of coach house and townhouse sales will pull the average down even if detached home values are holding steady and the reverse is true when more detached homes change hands. Anyone comparing month-to-month figures from a market report should read them with that in mind rather than assuming a shift in average price means every home type moved by the same amount. Always confirm current average prices and days on market with a local report or your agent rather than relying on a figure that may already be dated.

## What to Know Before Buying in Cornell

Before making an offer in Cornell, a few practical checks are worth doing regardless of how much you like the neighbourhood's design. Confirm which school each address is zoned for, since boundaries can shift and matter a great deal to families. Walk the laneway behind any home you are seriously considering, since laneway maintenance, snow clearing and shared access arrangements vary from block to block and are not always obvious from a listing photo.

It is also worth asking how a specific home's lot and laneway setup affects resale down the road. Buyers unfamiliar with new urbanist layouts sometimes hesitate at a narrower lot or a garage accessed from a lane, so understanding how that has affected comparable sales in Cornell specifically, rather than assuming it behaves like a standard Markham subdivision, will give you a more accurate read on value.
