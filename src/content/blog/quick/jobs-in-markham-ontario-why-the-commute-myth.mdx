## The short answer

* Markham's employment growth has kept pace with a long-standing city goal of about one job for every two residents, which is unusually high for a suburban municipality.
* The Stouffville GO line runs directly through Markham with several stations, giving commuters to downtown Toronto a transit option beyond Highway 404 or the DVP.
* Markham Centre and the Highway 7 corridor are where most new employment and housing density is concentrated, so proximity to those areas matters for commute times either way.
* Part time and full time openings in Markham span retail, warehousing, logistics, tech and corporate head offices and they show up daily on Job Bank, Indeed and similar sites.
* Housing costs and commute patterns both shift block by block in Markham, so the "long commute to Toronto" assumption often does not match the actual address.

## Why the commute myth doesn't hold up

Most people who are weighing a move to Markham arrive with one assumption: that it means trading a downtown Toronto commute for a longer downtown Toronto commute. That assumption made more sense twenty years ago, when Markham was mostly a bedroom community. It does not hold up as well today.

A report prepared for Markham's Development Services Committee put the city's total employment at approximately 184,750 jobs in 2023, up by 29,120 jobs since 2011. The city has also been explicit about its planning goal of maintaining roughly one job for every two residents, a ratio that is high for a municipality this size in the Greater Toronto Area. That ratio is the detail worth sitting with before you assume everyone in Markham drives to Toronto for work.

None of this means Markham has caught up to its own targets. The same report noted employment growth has lagged earlier forecasts, falling about 36,750 jobs short of what had been projected for 2021. But "behind forecast" and "no local jobs" are two very different situations and Markham is clearly the former.

## Jobs in Markham Ontario: what's actually out there

If you search jobs in Markham, Ontario on Job Bank, Indeed or SimplyHired on any given week, you will see a mix that reflects the city's real economic base: corporate and head office roles, technology and digital positions, finance and professional services, manufacturing, warehousing and logistics and a steady stream of retail and service jobs tied to Markham's shopping centres and business parks.

Job Bank's own search tool lets you filter by distance from a chosen point in Markham, which is a useful way to see what is realistically close to a specific neighbourhood rather than the city as a whole. Indeed and SimplyHired show similar patterns: full time roles are the most common listing type, but part time jobs in Markham, Ontario are also widely available, particularly in retail, warehousing and customer service.

If you are searching Indeed or Job Bank for Markham roles in a certain industry, searching by employer or sector rather than just the city name tends to surface more relevant results, since general searches return a very wide mix.

### Where the growth is concentrated

Most of Markham's future job and population growth is planned for two areas: Markham Centre, roughly bounded by Rodick Road, Kennedy Road, Highway 407 and the Highway 7 corridor and Langstaff Gateway near Yonge Street and Highway 7. These are the areas seeing high-density condo and commercial development and they are also where new employment is expected to land first. If working close to home matters to you, these are the areas to watch.

## How far is Markham from Toronto, really

How far is Markham from downtown Toronto depends heavily on which part of Markham you mean. The southern edge, near Highway 7, is closer to downtown than the northern parts near Stouffville Road. As a rough guide, driving distance runs from about 25 to 35 kilometres and the honest answer on travel time is that it depends enormously on when you leave.

Outside rush hour, a drive down Highway 404 or the Don Valley Parkway can take 30 to 40 minutes. During peak commuting windows, that same drive can stretch well past an hour. This is the part people moving from downtown often underestimate: it is not the distance that changes your life, it is the variability.

## Does the GO train go to Markham and is it any good

Yes, the Stouffville GO line runs through Markham, with stops including Markham, Centennial, Mount Joy and Unionville, connecting through to Union Station. This gives commuters a real alternative to driving, particularly for people working in the downtown core who want to avoid highway traffic entirely.

The commute from Markham to downtown Toronto by GO train tends to be more predictable than driving, though service frequency is heavier during peak hours and lighter in the evenings and on weekends. If your work schedule is standard nine-to-five, the GO line works well. If you need flexibility outside those windows, you will want to check current schedules carefully before committing to a GO-dependent routine.

For people wondering how to get to Markham from Toronto without a car at all, the combination of GO Train plus York Region Transit or VIVA rapid buses covers most of the city reasonably well, though it works better for commuting to fixed hubs like Markham Centre than for reaching more spread-out residential pockets.

## Cost of living: Markham vs Toronto

The cost of living in Markham compared to Toronto is a frequent question and the honest answer is that it varies by category and by exactly where in each city you are comparing. Housing is the biggest variable: depending on property type and neighbourhood, Markham can offer more space for a similar budget, though this gap has narrowed in some pockets and current listings should always be checked rather than assumed.

Everyday costs like groceries, utilities and insurance tend to be broadly similar across the GTA, though property tax rates and municipal fees differ by city and are worth confirming directly with each municipality before you budget around them.

## What this means if you're deciding where to live

If your job is downtown and flexible on schedule, the GO Train from one of Markham's Stouffville line stations can make the daily trip manageable and it removes the stress of highway variability. If your job has you driving at fixed peak hours, the commute from Markham to downtown Toronto will test your patience more days than not and that is worth being honest with yourself about before you buy.

If you are open to working closer to home, Markham's own employment base is worth a serious look before assuming you need to commute at all. With close to 185,000 jobs already in the city and more planned around Markham Centre and Langstaff Gateway, checking current postings on Job Bank or Indeed for your specific field is a reasonable first step, not a long shot.
