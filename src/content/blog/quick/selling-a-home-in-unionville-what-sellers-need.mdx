## The short answer

* Price your home using recent, truly comparable sales in Unionville, not city-wide averages or what a neighbour claims they got.
* Small, visible prep work (decluttering, fresh paint, minor repairs) almost always pays for itself; big renovations often don't.
* Homes within sought-after Markham school boundaries or close to Main Street Unionville can carry a genuine premium, so market that clearly.
* Spring and early fall usually bring more buyer traffic, but a well-prepared home sells in any season.
* Have your lawyer and agent line up the paperwork, status certificate (if applicable) and disclosures early so closing isn't rushed.

## Why pricing matters more than anything else

I've seen more Unionville sales stall over pricing than over anything wrong with the house itself. Buyers here are sophisticated. Many are comparing your listing against three or four others within a ten-minute drive, often on the same evening.

The right price comes from looking at what has actually sold, recently, in a similar style and size, in your part of Unionville. A detached home near the historic Main Street area doesn't compare cleanly to a townhome further east near Kennedy Road, even though both carry the "Unionville" name. Square footage, lot size, age and school catchment all move the number.

If you price too high hoping to "leave room to negotiate," the most common result is a listing that sits, picks up a stale reputation online and eventually sells for less than it would have if priced correctly from day one. Pricing right the first time is one of the clearest ways to protect your final sale price.

## What buyers actually pay attention to when selling a home in Unionville

Unionville draws a mix of buyers: families chasing specific school catchments, downsizers who want walkability to Main Street's shops and restaurants and investors watching the GO Train and highway access. Each group weighs things differently, but a few points come up again and again.

### School catchment

If your property falls within the boundary of a well-regarded Markham elementary or secondary school, say so plainly in your listing. Confirm current boundaries with the Markham District School Board or York Region District School Board before you promise anything in writing, since boundaries can shift.

### Proximity to Main Street Unionville

Being within walking distance of the historic Main Street, the pond and the shops carries real weight for a certain type of buyer. If that's you, lean into it with your photos and description rather than treating it as a minor detail.

### Condition versus potential

Move-in ready homes attract the widest buyer pool and usually the fastest offers. Homes that need work still sell, but they typically attract investors or renovators who price in the cost of the work, so expect offers that reflect that.

## Getting your home ready before you list

You don't need to gut a kitchen to sell well in Unionville. Most of the return comes from cheap, visible improvements.

* Declutter every room, including closets, since buyers open them.
* Fix the small things: a dripping tap, a cracked switch plate, a squeaky door. These signal how well the home has been maintained overall.
* Neutralize bold paint colours so buyers can picture their own furniture in the space.
* Deep clean, especially kitchens, bathrooms and windows. It's the cheapest upgrade available.
* Improve curb appeal: trimmed lawn, clear walkway, a tidy front entrance. First impressions start before the door opens.

Full staging isn't always necessary, but a professional walk-through consultation before photos are taken is usually worth the modest cost. An agent or stager will often spot things you've stopped noticing after years of living there.

## Timing your sale: does the season really matter?

Spring and early fall tend to bring more active buyers in Unionville, partly tied to the school year and partly to general market rhythm across the GTA. Winter listings see fewer showings on average, but they also face less competition from other sellers.

Summer can be quieter due to vacations, though a well-priced, well-presented home still moves. The honest answer is that timing matters less than most sellers assume. A home priced and presented correctly will attract offers in almost any month; a poorly priced one will sit no matter the season.

## Costs of selling in Unionville: what to budget for

Sellers are sometimes surprised by the number of smaller costs that add up around a sale. None of these figures are fixed and you should confirm current numbers with your agent and lawyer, but here's what to plan around.

| Cost                            | What it covers                                        | How it's usually determined                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Real estate commission          | Listing agent and buyer's agent compensation          | Negotiated between you and your agent, typically a percentage of sale price |
| Legal fees                      | Reviewing the agreement, handling closing, title work | Flat fee or hourly rate quoted by your real estate lawyer                   |
| Mortgage discharge/penalty      | Paying out your existing mortgage early               | Set by your lender; ask for a payout statement before listing               |
| Staging or minor repairs        | Preparing the home for photos and showings            | Varies widely; get quotes from a stager or contractor                       |
| Status certificate fee (condos) | Document required for condo sales                     | Set by the condo corporation, confirm current fee with them                 |

## What to do next

If you're seriously thinking about selling a home in Unionville in the next few months, start with these steps this week:

* Ask a local agent for a comparative market analysis based on recent, truly comparable Unionville sales, not a generic city-wide estimate.
* Walk through your home with fresh eyes or ask a friend to and make a short list of the small fixes and decluttering that stand out.
* Call your mortgage lender for a current payout statement so you know your numbers before you set a price.
* If you're in a condo, request a status certificate early so it's ready when you need it.
* Confirm your school catchment and any other neighbourhood details you plan to market, rather than relying on memory.
