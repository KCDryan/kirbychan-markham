## The short answer

* Unionville's historic Main Street and Toogood Pond give it a walkability that most of Markham and most of the GTA, simply does not have.
* Housing stock ranges from 19th century cottages to 1980s and 90s executive homes to newer townhouses and low rise condos.
* Parts of the neighbourhood fall inside a heritage conservation district, which affects what you can change on the exterior of a home.
* Schools in the area, including Unionville High School, are a major reason families pay a premium to live here.
* Commuting downtown is workable but should be tested at rush hour before you commit, whether by car or GO Transit.

## What Makes Unionville Different From the Rest of Markham

Drive through most of Markham and you get wide arterial roads, big box plazas and subdivisions built around the car. Unionville breaks that pattern. Its Main Street still has the scale of a 19th century village: narrow, tree-lined, lined with restored storefronts, cafes and small shops you can actually walk between.

That is not an accident. Unionville started as a separate village long before Markham grew around it and a lot of the original street grid and building stock survived because the community fought to protect it. The result today is one of the few places in this part of the GTA where you can park the car for a weekend and do everything on foot: coffee, groceries, a walk by the water, dinner.

Toogood Pond adds to that. It is a genuine park, not a retention pond dressed up with a bench, with walking trails, mature trees and enough space that it feels like a break from the city rather than a strip of green between subdivisions. For buyers who grew up wanting a Main Street and a pond within walking distance, this is one of the only places in Markham that delivers both.

## Housing Options When You're Living in Unionville Markham

The housing stock here is more varied than people expect. Close to the Main Street heritage core you will find original century homes and worker's cottages, some beautifully restored, some needing real work. These carry character that newer builds cannot replicate, but they also come with older mechanicals, smaller lots and heritage rules that limit what you can change.

Move a few streets out and you hit the executive detached homes built through the 1980s and 90s, generally larger lots, attached garages and layouts suited to families who want space without leaving the neighbourhood. Further from the core, newer townhouses and low rise condo buildings give buyers a lower entry price while staying inside Unionville's boundaries.

This mix means the neighbourhood does not have one single price point or one single buyer profile. A retired couple downsizing from a large detached home, a young family stretching for the school catchment and a first time buyer looking at a townhouse can all end up living in Unionville Markham within a few blocks of each other.

### What to Expect Street by Street

* Streets directly off Main Street: older homes, smaller lots, heritage review on exterior changes.
* Streets north toward Toogood Pond: mix of character homes and larger executive properties.
* Streets further from the core: newer townhouses, condos and detached homes with fewer heritage restrictions.

## Understanding the Unionville Heritage Conservation District

A meaningful part of Unionville's Main Street area and the streets immediately around it sit inside a heritage conservation district managed by the City of Markham. This designation exists to protect the historic character that makes the neighbourhood what it is, but it also means real restrictions for owners.

If you buy a home inside the district, exterior changes such as new windows, additions, roofing materials, fences and even paint colour in some cases can require review and approval before work starts. This is not a reason to avoid the area, plenty of owners live happily inside these rules for years, but it is something to confirm before you buy if you are planning renovations.

Not every street commonly associated with "Unionville" actually falls inside the formal heritage boundary. Always ask your agent or check directly with the City of Markham planning department to confirm whether a specific address is inside the district before you make renovation plans part of your offer.

## Schools and Family Life in the Area

Unionville falls under the York Region District School Board for public education, with French Catholic options available through the York Catholic District School Board. Unionville High School has a strong reputation and is a genuine draw for families relocating from elsewhere in the GTA specifically for the school catchment.

Beyond the schools themselves, the walkability of the neighbourhood matters for families in a practical way. Kids can walk or bike to school, to the pond, to Main Street for an ice cream, in a way that is not really possible in most newer Markham subdivisions built around collector roads.

## Commuting From Unionville to Toronto

Distance to downtown Toronto by car typically runs somewhere between 35 and 50 minutes depending on traffic, time of day and exact route. That is a reasonable commute by GTA standards, but rush hour on Highway 404 or through the 401 corridor can push it longer, so test the drive yourself before assuming a number.

GO Transit and York Region Transit both serve the area, giving commuters an alternative to driving. Travel time on transit depends heavily on which line and station you use, so anyone commuting daily should map out their actual route and timing rather than relying on a general estimate.

## What to Check Before You Buy in Unionville

Before writing an offer on any home in this pocket of Markham, confirm a few things that are easy to overlook when you are focused on the charm of the street. Heritage status, school catchment boundaries and flood mapping near Toogood Pond are the three that come up most often.

* Confirm whether the specific address falls inside the heritage conservation district with the City of Markham.
* Verify current school catchment boundaries directly with the York Region District School Board, since boundaries can shift.
* Ask about flood plain mapping for any property near Toogood Pond or the Rouge River tributaries.
* Get a home inspection that specifically flags original mechanicals, knob and tube wiring or aging foundations in older homes.
* Ask your agent for a recent comparison of sold prices by street, since values can differ significantly a few blocks apart.
