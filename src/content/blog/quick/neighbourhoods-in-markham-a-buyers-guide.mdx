## The short answer

* [Unionville](/unionville-markham/) has a historic Main Street and mature streets and homes there tend to price above many newer subdivisions.
* Cornell and Wismer Commons offer newer housing stock, though lot sizes and street layouts differ noticeably between the two.
* Milliken Mills, Berczy and Markham Village are established areas worth comparing against the newer communities.
* Condo buyers and commuters usually focus near Highway 7, Highway 404 or the GO stations for access into Toronto.
* School boundaries and transit plans change, so confirm both against the specific address rather than the general area.

## Understanding the neighbourhoods in Markham before you shop

Markham is a large city and the neighbourhoods in Markham vary more than people expect. Someone comparing a condo near Highway 7 to a detached home in Cachet is really comparing two different lifestyles, not just two price points. Before you start touring homes, it helps to know roughly which part of the city fits how you actually live day to day.

I usually ask buyers three questions first: where do you work, do you want new or established and how will you check the school for each address. The answers usually point toward two or three areas worth a closer look, which saves a lot of wasted weekends. The [neighbourhood guides](/neighbourhoods/) are a good place to start.

## Unionville: the classic choice

Unionville is probably the most recognized of Markham's neighbourhoods, built around a historic Main Street. The City of Markham treats the area as a heritage environment that its planning policies aim to protect. That gives it a settled, walkable feel that is hard to replicate in newer subdivisions.

Homes in Unionville typically sell at a premium compared to similarly sized homes elsewhere in Markham and buyers should expect competition on well-located listings. If walkability matters more to you than square footage per dollar, it is worth the look. Our [Unionville guide](/unionville-markham/) has the details.

## Cornell and Wismer Commons: newer communities, different designs

Cornell, in east Markham, is a planned New Urbanist community. Streets are narrower, homes often sit close to the sidewalk with front porches and many properties use rear laneway garages. Some buyers love the streetscape; others find the tighter lots and street parking rules a real adjustment. See the [Cornell guide](/cornell-markham/).

Wismer Commons, in north Markham, follows a more conventional suburban layout with driveways. Comparing the two areas is a good exercise in figuring out what kind of "new" you actually want. The [Wismer guide](/wismer-markham/) covers it and our guide to [new construction in Markham](/new-construction-markham/) explains what to check on a newer build.

### What to check before choosing a newer community

* Confirm the school for the exact address with the school board, since boundaries can shift as new schools open.
* Ask whether nearby land is still being developed, which can mean construction noise for a few years.
* Check whether the home has laneway or front-drive parking, since this affects both daily life and resale appeal.

## Established areas: Milliken Mills, Berczy and Markham Village

Not every buyer wants brand new and Markham has several established neighbourhoods with mature streets and a settled feel. Milliken Mills sits along the southern edge of the city next to Toronto and has a mix of housing ages. Read the [Milliken Mills guide](/milliken-mills-markham/) for its transit links.

Berczy Village, in north Markham, has newer detached and townhome stock. Markham Village, near the older town centre, has some of the city's original housing stock along with newer infill. Both have their own pages: [Berczy Village](/berczy-village-markham/) and [Markham Village](/markham-village-markham/).

## Condo living near Highway 7 and the GO corridor

If you are not set on a house, Markham's condo market clusters mainly around Highway 7, near [Downtown Markham](/downtown-markham/) and close to Unionville GO and Markham GO stations. These spots suit buyers who want a transit commute into Toronto and shops and restaurants nearby.

Condo fees, building age and reserve fund health vary a lot between buildings even in the same neighbourhood, so this is one area where a building-by-building comparison matters more than a general neighbourhood reputation.

## Commute and transit: what actually changes by area

Markham has both Highway 404 and Highway 7 running through it. Actual drive times into Toronto or across Markham itself change significantly depending on which side of the city you are on and what time you travel.

GO Transit's Stouffville line serves several Markham stations, including Centennial, Markham and Mount Joy, according to Metrolinx. York Region Transit and Viva cover local routes with varying frequency. If commuting matters, test the drive or transit trip yourself at the time you would actually be travelling, rather than relying on a map estimate.

## How to compare Markham neighbourhoods for your own shortlist

Once you have narrowed the neighbourhoods in Markham down to a shortlist, the next step is comparing actual recent sales rather than asking prices. Asking prices tell you what a seller hopes for, sold prices tell you what buyers are actually paying and the gap between the two can be significant depending on how competitive a particular street or building has been. If you are buying your first home, our [first-time buyer guide](/first-time-home-buyers-markham/) covers the steps.

It also helps to visit at different times of day. A street that feels quiet on a Tuesday morning showing can feel very different during a Saturday afternoon with school traffic or event parking nearby.

## What to do next

* Write down your top three priorities (commute, school boundary, new versus established, budget) before touring anything.
* Ask for a list of recent sold prices, not just listings, in each neighbourhood you're considering.
* Confirm the school for any specific address using the board's school locator rather than relying on general reputation.
* Drive or take transit through the commute you would actually do, at the time you would actually do it.
* Get pre-approved so you know which neighbourhoods are realistically within reach before you fall for a house that isn't.
