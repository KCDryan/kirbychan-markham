## The short answer

* Unionville and Cathedraltown carry a price premium for their character, mature streets and school reputation.
* Cornell and Wismer Commons offer newer housing stock, though lot sizes and street layouts differ noticeably between the two.
* Milliken Mills, Berczy and Markham Village suit buyers who want established neighbourhoods without paying top dollar.
* Condo buyers and commuters usually focus near Highway 7, Highway 404 or the GO stations for easy access into Toronto.
* School boundaries and transit plans change, so confirm both against the specific address rather than the general area.

## Understanding the neighbourhoods in Markham before you shop

Markham is a large city and the neighbourhoods in Markham vary more than people expect. Someone comparing a condo near Highway 7 to a detached home in Cachet is really comparing two different lifestyles, not just two price points. Before you start touring homes, it helps to know roughly which part of the city fits how you actually live day to day.

I usually ask buyers three questions first: where do you work, do you want new or established and how important are specific schools. The answers usually point toward two or three areas worth a closer look, which saves a lot of wasted weekends.

## Unionville: the classic choice

Unionville is probably the most recognized of Markham's neighbourhoods, built around a historic Main Street with heritage buildings, a pond and mature trees. It has a reputation for strong schools and a settled, walkable feel that is hard to replicate in newer subdivisions.

That reputation comes at a cost. Homes in Unionville typically sell at a premium compared to similarly sized homes elsewhere in Markham and buyers should expect competition on well-located listings. If walkability and long-term resale confidence matter more to you than square footage per dollar, it is worth the look.

## Cornell and Wismer Commons: newer communities, different designs

Cornell, in the southeast, was one of the first large-scale New Urbanist communities in the Greater Toronto Area. Streets are narrower, homes often sit close to the sidewalk with front porches and many properties use rear laneway garages. Some buyers love the streetscape; others find the tighter lots and street parking rules a real adjustment.

Wismer Commons, in the north end near Bur Oak, follows a more conventional suburban layout with larger lots and driveways. It has grown quickly over the past two decades and now has a good mix of schools, parks and plazas. Comparing these two areas is a good exercise in figuring out what kind of "new" you actually want.

### What to check before choosing a newer community

* Confirm which school the address is zoned for, since boundaries can shift as new schools open.
* Ask whether nearby land is still being developed, which can mean construction noise or new competition for a few years.
* Check whether the home has laneway or front-drive parking, since this affects both daily life and resale appeal.

## Established family areas: Milliken Mills, Berczy and Markham Village

Not every buyer wants brand new and Markham has several established neighbourhoods that offer mature trees, larger lots relative to price and a settled community feel. Milliken Mills, along the southern edge of the city, has a mix of housing ages and generally trades at lower prices than the newer north Markham communities.

Berczy, in the northeast, has a strong following among families for its schools and parks and it sits between the more expensive Unionville area and the newer Cornell and Wismer developments in terms of price. Markham Village, near the older downtown core, offers some of the city's original housing stock along with newer infill, which appeals to buyers who want character without Unionville's premium.

## Condo living near Highway 7 and the GO corridor

If you are not set on a house, Markham's condo market clusters mainly around Highway 7, near Downtown Markham (the area around Warden and Highway 7) and close to Unionville GO and Markham GO stations. These spots suit commuters heading into Toronto, buyers who want to be near restaurants and shops and anyone looking to scale down from a larger property.

Condo fees, building age and reserve fund health vary a lot between buildings even in the same neighbourhood, so this is one area where a building-by-building comparison matters more than a general neighbourhood reputation.

## Commute and transit: what actually changes by area

Markham sits along both Highway 404 and Highway 7 and much of the city has reasonable access to Highway 407 as well. Even so, actual drive times into Toronto or across Markham itself change significantly depending on which side of the city you are on and what time you travel.

GO Transit's Stouffville line runs through several Markham stations and York Region Transit and VIVA cover local routes with varying frequency. If commuting matters, test the drive or transit trip yourself at the time you would actually be travelling, rather than relying on a map estimate.

## How to compare Markham neighbourhoods for your own shortlist

Once you have narrowed the neighbourhoods in Markham down to a shortlist, the next step is comparing actual recent sales rather than asking prices. Asking prices tell you what a seller hopes for, sold prices tell you what buyers are actually paying and the gap between the two can be significant depending on how competitive a particular street or building has been.

It also helps to visit at different times of day. A street that feels quiet on a Tuesday morning showing can feel very different during a Saturday afternoon with school traffic or event parking nearby.

## What to do next

* Write down your top three priorities (commute, schools, new versus established, budget) before touring anything.
* Ask for a list of recent sold prices, not just listings, in each neighbourhood you're considering.
* Confirm the school boundary for any specific address directly with the school board rather than relying on general reputation.
* Drive or take transit through the commute you would actually do, at the time you would actually do it.
* Get pre-approved so you know which neighbourhoods are realistically within reach before you fall for a house that isn't.
