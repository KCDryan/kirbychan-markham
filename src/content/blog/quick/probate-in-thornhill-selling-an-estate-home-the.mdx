## The short answer

* An estate usually needs a court-issued certificate before real estate can be legally sold, unless the home was owned jointly with a surviving spouse.
* Estate administration tax is calculated on the value of the whole estate, including the house and is paid when the certificate is issued.
* You can prepare, price and list a probate home before the certificate arrives, but the closing date is usually set later to allow time for it.
* An up-to-date, unbiased opinion of value matters for the court application, for the beneficiaries and for pricing the sale correctly.
* A lawyer handles the legal filing; a local real estate agent handles the pricing, condition, marketing and sale itself and the two roles work best in step with each other.

## What Probate Actually Means for a Thornhill Property

When someone dies owning a home in their own name, that property doesn't automatically transfer to their children or beneficiaries. Someone has to be legally recognized as having the authority to deal with the estate's assets, including the real estate. That's what probate does: the court reviews the will (or the situation if there isn't one) and issues a certificate confirming who the estate trustee is.

Here in Thornhill, this matters because most homes in the area, whether it's a bungalow near Bathurst and Centre, a larger property up near John Street or a townhome closer to Yonge, are held in one person's name or jointly. If it was held jointly with a spouse who is still living, the property usually passes to them directly without probate. If it was held solely by the person who passed away, probate is almost always required before a title transfer or sale can be registered.

Land registry offices in Ontario will not register a transfer of a solely-owned property without seeing that certificate. That's the practical reason this process can't be skipped, no matter how clear the will seems or how much everyone in the family agrees on what should happen.

## How the Probate Process Works Step by Step

The estate trustee named in the will (or a family member who applies to be appointed if there's no will) works with an estate lawyer to prepare an application to the Superior Court of Justice. This includes the original will, a death certificate, an inventory of assets including the estimated value of the home and the required forms.

The court reviews the application and, if everything is in order, issues a certificate of appointment of estate trustee. In York Region, this process commonly takes a couple of months for straightforward estates, though it can run longer if the court is backed up, if the application has gaps or if a beneficiary raises an objection.

Once the certificate is issued, the estate trustee has the legal standing to sign listing agreements, accept offers and complete a sale, exactly as an owner would, but on behalf of the estate.

### What Slows It Down

* A will that's unclear, contested or missing entirely.
* Multiple beneficiaries who disagree on the executor or on what should happen with the property.
* Incomplete paperwork or an estimated home value that hasn't been properly documented.
* Court processing times, which fluctuate and are outside anyone's control.

## Can You List a Home Before Probate Is Granted?

This is one of the most common questions I get from families dealing with a Thornhill estate. In most cases, yes. You can get the home ready, have it professionally evaluated and even list it for sale while the probate application is working its way through the court. What you generally can't do is close the sale until the certificate is in hand, unless the estate lawyer confirms an exception applies to your situation.

Doing this in parallel, rather than waiting for probate to finish before lifting a finger, saves real time. Thornhill homes that sit empty for months while paperwork is sorted out often need more work by the time they're ready to list: gardens get overgrown, systems that aren't used can develop issues and the property can start to look neglected to buyers driving by.

The key is setting expectations properly in the listing. Buyers and their agents need to know upfront that the closing is conditional on probate being granted and the closing date is usually set further out than a typical resale transaction to build in that buffer.

## Estate Administration Tax and Other Costs

When the court issues the certificate, the estate pays what's called estate administration tax, based on the total value of the estate's assets at the date of death. That includes the home's value along with bank accounts, investments and other property. Ontario has a reduced rate on the first portion of the estate's value and a higher rate above that threshold, but the exact figures change from time to time, so confirm the current rate with the estate lawyer or Ontario's Ministry of Finance rather than going by an older number you may have heard.

Beyond that tax, expect legal fees for the probate application itself and the usual costs of selling a home: any repairs or updates needed before listing, real estate commission, legal fees for the closing and adjustments at closing for things like property tax. None of these are unique to probate sales, but families are often not expecting the estate administration tax specifically, since it's not something most people encounter when selling their own home.

## Pricing and Preparing an Estate Home for Sale

Estate homes in Thornhill often haven't been updated in a while, sometimes for decades, because the previous owner lived there a long time. That's not a bad thing for buyers looking for a project or an opportunity, but it does change how the property should be priced and marketed compared to a freshly renovated resale listing nearby.

I usually walk the property with the estate trustee early, before any major decisions are made, to talk through what's worth fixing, what isn't and how the home compares to what else has sold recently in the same pocket of Thornhill. Sometimes a light cosmetic refresh, decluttering and professional photos make a real difference. Other times, the smarter move is to price it clearly as-is and let the numbers speak for themselves, especially if the estate doesn't have cash on hand to invest in updates.

An accurate opinion of value also supports the probate application itself, since the court wants a realistic figure for the estate's inventory of assets, not a guess.

## Working With an Estate Lawyer and Real Estate Agent Together

Probate and real estate are two separate jobs that need to happen in step. The estate lawyer handles the court filing, the tax calculation and the legal transfer of title. The real estate agent handles pricing, preparing the home, marketing it properly and managing the sale process itself, including making sure buyers understand the timeline.

When these two sides communicate well, families avoid a lot of the stress that comes with estate sales. I've worked with several Thornhill families through this process and the smoothest ones are the cases where the lawyer and I were talking early, so nobody was surprised by a closing date, a tax bill or a delay.
