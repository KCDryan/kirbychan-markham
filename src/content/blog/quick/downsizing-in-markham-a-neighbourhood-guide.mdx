## The short answer

* Many of the downsizers I talk with want to stay in Markham to keep their doctor, dentist and social circle.
* Unionville has a heritage main street and the Rouge Valley Trail along Toogood Pond, which suits people who want to walk to errands.
* Cornell has newer homes and the Cornell Community Centre & Library.
* Berczy and Cathedraltown have newer freehold townhomes. Cathedraltown has some low rise condos. Highway 404 is the usual driving route for both.
* Milliken and Wismer are worth a look if you want to live near family. Milliken is at the south end of the city and Wismer is in the north.
* The financial side, timing your sale against your purchase, matters as much as picking the right area.

## Why Markham Homeowners Downsize in the First Place

Nobody downsizes just because a house got too big. The house was always this size. What changed is the life inside it: kids moved out, stairs became a hassle or the yard work that used to feel satisfying now feels like a chore with no reward.

Some clients come to me after a health scare in the family made a two-storey with a finished basement feel impractical. Others are simply tired of paying property tax and utilities on square footage they visit twice a year. Whatever the reason, [downsizing in Markham](/downsizing-markham/) works best when you decide what you are downsizing away from before you decide where to go.

## Unionville: For People Who Want to Keep Walking Everywhere

[Unionville](/unionville-markham/) is the neighbourhood I point people toward when they tell me they still want a "village" feeling, just without the mortgage that comes with a big lot. The historic Main Street and the Rouge Valley Trail along Toogood Pond are close together. There are mid-rise condos toward Downtown Markham so you can move from a four-bedroom house to a two-bedroom unit and still be near a coffee shop.

Prices here tend to run higher than in many other Markham pockets, so look at current listings and recent sales before you set a budget. Unionville GO is on the Stouffville line, which is worth knowing if you still travel downtown.

### What many downsizers like about it

* The chance to walk to errands rather than drive.
* A main street with patios and community events.
* A mix of housing types, from heritage homes to townhomes and condos.

## Cornell: Newer Builds With a Small-Town Layout

[Cornell](/cornell-markham/) is a planned community built on a grid, with front porches and rear lane garages. A lot of my downsizing clients moving from older parts of Markham look at the townhomes and semis here because newer homes can mean fewer surprise repairs, although an inspection is still worth doing.

It sits on the eastern edge of the city, so it is further from Unionville and the downtown core. The Cornell Community Centre & Library on Bur Oak Avenue, beside Markham Stouffville Hospital, offers a pool, fitness centre and library under one roof. If you are downsizing in Markham specifically to reduce maintenance, Cornell is worth a serious look.

## Berczy and Cathedraltown: Newer Homes, Easy Highway Access

[Berczy Village](/berczy-village-markham/) and [Cathedraltown](/cathedraltown-markham/) are newer north Markham neighbourhoods with Highway 404 as the usual driving route. Berczy has very little condo stock, so a freehold townhome is the main lower maintenance option there. Cathedraltown has freehold townhomes, smaller semis and some low rise condos.

Neither has a GO station of its own, so you will likely drive more than walk. For downsizers who still want a car-based lifestyle but want to shed yard work, this can be a good fit.

## Milliken and Wismer: Staying Close to Family

A good number of my downsizing clients are not chasing a lifestyle, they are chasing proximity to their adult children and grandchildren. Milliken Mills sits at the south end of Markham against the Toronto border and has townhomes and condo apartments along its main corridors. Wismer is in the north and is mostly newer family housing with limited condo stock.

If your main motivation for downsizing in Markham is staying near family rather than finding a walkable village, these two areas deserve a closer look even though they get less attention than Unionville or Cornell.

## House to Condo: What Actually Changes Day to Day

The neighbourhood matters, but so does the housing type you choose within it. Moving from a house to a condo changes more than your address, it changes your monthly costs, your storage and how much control you have over renovations.

* Condo fees can replace some of what you used to spend on lawn care and snow removal, depending on what the corporation covers, so compare the fee against your actual past spending, not just against a mortgage payment.
* Storage shrinks significantly, so plan to part with furniture and seasonal items well before moving day, not the week of.
* Condo boards have rules about renovations, pets and rentals. A [status certificate](https://www.condoauthorityontario.ca/condo-living/corporate-records/status-certificates/) can include the declaration, by-laws, rules, budget and a statement on the reserve fund, so read it before you fall in love with a unit.
* Bungalows and bungalow-style townhomes give you a smaller footprint without giving up a yard entirely, which some downsizers prefer over a condo.

## Timing the Sale and the Purchase

The neighbourhood decision gets a lot of attention, but the financial sequencing is where downsizing plans actually run into trouble. You are usually selling one property and buying another at close to the same time and the gap between the two closings determines how stressful the move feels.

Some sellers close their sale first and use the proceeds to buy outright, which avoids financing altogether but means you need somewhere to stay in between, sometimes with a rent-back arrangement with the buyer of your old house. Others negotiate a longer closing date on their sale so it lines up with their new purchase. A mortgage broker can also arrange short-term bridge financing if the dates do not quite match, though it comes with its own costs that should be confirmed before you rely on it. See my [seller's guide to downsizing in Markham](/blog/downsizing-in-markham-a-sellers-guide/) for more on timing.

Whichever route you take, get your agent and mortgage broker talking to each other early, not after you have already made an offer on a new place.

## What Buyers of Your Current House Are Actually Looking For

If you are downsizing out of a larger detached home in Markham, the buyer for that house may well be a growing family. That changes how you should present the property.

* Yard space matters to many buyers, so show it well in listing photos. School boundaries are set by the school boards, so buyers need to confirm them for the exact address.
* Basement condition matters, since some buyers plan to finish or rent it.
* Neutral, well-lit staging usually photographs better than personal decor.
* A pre-listing inspection can prevent a buyer's inspector from finding something that stalls the deal at the worst possible moment.

## What to Do Next

Start with a walk through your own house and make a short, honest list of what you actually use versus what you keep out of habit. That list will tell you more about how much space you need than any square footage number will.

Then spend a Saturday visiting two or three of the neighbourhoods above in person, not just online. Notice how far you would actually walk, not how far the listing says you could walk. Finally, get a rough value estimate on your current home through the [home valuation page](/home-valuation/) and a mortgage pre-approval on the smaller purchase before you fall in love with a specific unit, so you know your numbers work before your heart is involved.
