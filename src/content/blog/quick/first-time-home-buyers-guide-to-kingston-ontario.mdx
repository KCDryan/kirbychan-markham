## The short answer

* You will typically need at least 5% down for homes under a certain price point, with higher minimums as price rises.
* Get a mortgage pre-approval before you start viewing homes so you know your real budget.
* Budget an extra 1.5% to 4% of the purchase price for closing costs on top of your down payment.
* Kingston has distinct pockets, from downtown heritage streets to newer subdivisions in the west end and Amherstview and prices vary a lot between them.
* Ontario and federal programs can help with the down payment or reduce upfront costs, but each has rules you need to check carefully.
* A home inspection is not legally required in Ontario but it is one of the smartest few hundred dollars a first time buyer can spend.

## How Much Do You Actually Need to Save?

Most first time buyers focus on the down payment and forget the rest. There are really two piles of money to plan for: the down payment itself and the closing costs that show up on top of it.

In Canada, minimum down payment rules are set federally and scale with price. Homes under a certain threshold require a smaller minimum down payment and that minimum rises for higher-priced homes. Kingston's average home price sits comfortably below Toronto's, which means many local buyers can get into the market with a more modest down payment than they might expect. Confirm the current thresholds with your lender or the CMHC website, since these figures are reviewed periodically.

Closing costs are the part people underestimate. These usually include:

* Land transfer tax (Ontario charges this provincially; Kingston does not add a separate municipal land transfer tax, unlike Toronto)
* Legal fees for your real estate lawyer
* Home inspection fee
* Title insurance
* Adjustments for prepaid property tax or utilities

A common rule of thumb is to budget 1.5% to 4% of the purchase price for these costs, but ask your lawyer for an exact quote once you have an accepted offer.

## First Time Buyer Programs Worth Knowing About

Several programs exist to help first time buyers in Ontario and it is worth checking your eligibility for each before you assume you cannot afford to buy.

### The First Home Savings Account (FHSA)

This federal registered account lets first time buyers save with tax-deductible contributions and tax-free withdrawals when used toward a home. It combines features of an RRSP and a TFSA. Annual and lifetime contribution limits apply, so check the current numbers on the CRA website before opening one.

### Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)

This allows you to withdraw funds from your RRSP to put toward a down payment, without paying tax on the withdrawal, as long as you repay it over time under CRA rules. The withdrawal limit has changed in recent years, so confirm the current cap directly with the CRA or your lender.

### Land Transfer Tax Rebate

Ontario offers a rebate on the provincial land transfer tax for qualifying first time buyers, which can reduce or eliminate that cost on a typical Kingston purchase price. Your lawyer will apply this for you at closing, but ask about it early so you understand what it does and does not cover.

### First Time Home Buyer Incentive Programs

Program availability and rules change fairly often at the federal and provincial level. Rather than rely on outdated information, ask your mortgage broker or lender which programs are currently active and whether you qualify based on your income and the purchase price.

## Getting Pre-Approved Before You Search

A mortgage pre-approval tells you three things: roughly how much you can borrow, what interest rate you might lock in and what your monthly payment could look like. It is different from a full approval, which happens after you have a specific property and a signed offer.

Walking into the Kingston market without a pre-approval is a common mistake. You risk falling for a house above your real budget or missing out because you cannot move fast enough when a good listing appears. Speak to a mortgage broker or your bank early, even before you start browsing listings seriously.

## What a Home Inspection Actually Tells You

Ontario does not require a home inspection by law, but skipping one is a gamble few first time buyers should take, especially with Kingston's mix of older housing stock downtown and near the university.

An inspector checks the roof, foundation, electrical panel, plumbing, furnace and signs of water damage or pest issues. For older Kingston homes, this can flag things like knob and tube wiring, aging cast iron pipes or foundation cracks that are expensive to fix. For a newer build in Amherstview or the west end, the inspection is shorter but still worthwhile since even new construction has deficiencies.

Expect to pay a few hundred dollars for a standard inspection. It is one of the few costs in this process that is entirely optional and entirely worth it.

## Making an Offer in Kingston's Market

Offer strategy in Kingston shifts with the season and the specific property. A well-priced home in a popular area like Amherstview or a walkable downtown street can attract multiple offers, while a home that has sat on the market for a while may leave more room to negotiate.

Conditions matter as much as price. First time buyers should think carefully before waiving a financing or inspection condition just to make an offer more competitive. Your agent can tell you, based on current activity for that specific type of property, whether conditions are realistic or whether you are likely up against other offers.

## Closing Day: What Actually Happens

Closing is the legal transfer of ownership. Your lawyer handles the paperwork, transfers funds, registers the property in your name and gives you the keys. In the days leading up to closing, you will:

* Do a final walkthrough of the property
* Arrange home insurance, which your lender will require proof of before releasing funds
* Confirm your down payment funds are ready to transfer to your lawyer
* Set up utilities in your name for the possession date

Most first time buyers are surprised by how much of closing day is handled by the lawyer rather than the agent or lender. Choose a real estate lawyer early, ideally as soon as your offer is accepted, so there is no scramble near the closing date.
