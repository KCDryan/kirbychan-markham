## The short answer

* Unionville is a historic village-turned-neighbourhood inside the City of Markham, roughly 30 to 40 minutes from downtown Toronto by car or GO Train.
* Main Street Unionville is the heart of the neighbourhood, with heritage buildings, independent restaurants and seasonal events like the Unionville Festival.
* Schools in the area are well regarded within the York Region District School Board, which is one of the main reasons families target this part of Markham.
* Housing stock ranges from century homes near Main Street to newer townhomes and detached houses on the neighbourhood's edges, with prices reflecting that mix.
* Toogood Pond Park and the Toonerville Trail give residents walking and cycling access without leaving the community.
* Commuters can use the Unionville GO Station on the Stouffville line for a direct trip into Union Station.

## What makes Unionville different from the rest of Markham

Markham is a large city with several distinct pockets and Unionville is the one most people picture when they hear the name. It grew from an actual 19th-century village and the historic Main Street was preserved rather than demolished as the surrounding area developed. That decision is the reason the neighbourhood has the look and feel it has today.

Walk along Main Street and you will see restored heritage buildings now housing cafes, restaurants and small shops, sitting alongside a millpond and mature trees. It reads more like a small Ontario town centre than a suburb of a fast-growing city and that contrast is a big part of the appeal for buyers who want character in their surroundings.

Outside the historic core, Unionville extends into more conventional suburban streets: detached houses from different building eras, some townhome clusters and pockets of newer construction closer to the edges of the neighbourhood boundary. So when people talk about living in Unionville, they are usually talking about two slightly different experiences depending on which street they end up on.

## Schools and family life in Unionville

Unionville consistently comes up when Markham families discuss school catchments. The neighbourhood falls within the York Region District School Board and several of the schools serving the area have strong reputations locally.

That said, school boundaries shift from time to time and a school's reputation from a few years ago is not a guarantee of its current ranking or catchment area. If school assignment is a deciding factor in your purchase, confirm the exact designated school for the specific address, not just the general neighbourhood, through the York Region District School Board directly before you write an offer.

Beyond schools, the neighbourhood has the practical family infrastructure you would expect: parks, community centres, libraries and a low-traffic feel on most residential streets away from Kennedy Road and Highway 7.

## Getting around: commuting from Unionville

One of the strongest arguments for living in Unionville, Markham is the commute flexibility. The Unionville GO Station sits on the Stouffville line and runs directly into Union Station, which makes downtown Toronto commuting realistic without a car for anyone working near the downtown core.

For drivers, Highway 407 and Highway 7 both provide access west and east and Kennedy Road runs north-south through the area. Actual drive times into Toronto vary a lot depending on time of day, so if you are commuting by car daily, test the drive at your actual commute times before assuming the map estimate holds up.

Within the neighbourhood itself, many day-to-day errands are walkable if you live close to Main Street. Further out, you will likely want a car for groceries, big-box shopping and getting to Unionville GO Station itself.

### Public transit beyond GO

York Region Transit and the Viva rapid transit service both run routes through Markham, connecting Unionville to other parts of York Region including Markham Centre and Richmond Hill. This matters if you are working somewhere in the region rather than commuting straight into Toronto.

## Parks, trails and things to do in Unionville

Toogood Pond Park is the standout green space in the neighbourhood. It is popular for walking, especially in autumn when the tree colour draws visitors from outside the area and it connects into a broader trail network.

The Toonerville Trail links several Markham neighbourhoods together and gives Unionville residents a way to walk or cycle without relying on a car for shorter trips. The Markham Museum sits nearby as well, giving families an easy outing that ties into the area's history.

Main Street itself functions as a destination, not just a through street. The Unionville Festival, held each June, brings crowds for music, food and craft vendors and it is one of the more established community events in the region.

## Home prices and housing stock in Unionville

Housing in Unionville is more varied than its reputation suggests. Near the historic core, you will find older detached homes, some dating back well over a century, sitting on established lots with mature trees. Moving outward, the housing mix shifts toward homes built from the 1980s through the 2000s, along with newer townhome developments closer to Kennedy Road and the GO Station.

Because of the location and the heritage appeal, Unionville generally prices above the Markham-wide average, though the gap depends heavily on which street and which era of home you are comparing. ## Who tends to move to Unionville

Families chasing school catchments make up a large share of buyers here, along with move-up buyers who already know Markham and want the historic streetscape specifically. Downsizers also show interest in the newer townhome product near the edges of the neighbourhood, since it offers a lower-maintenance option without leaving the area they know.

Commuters who work downtown but want more space and a slower pace than a Toronto neighbourhood offers are another common buyer profile, largely because the GO Station makes that trade-off workable on a daily basis.
