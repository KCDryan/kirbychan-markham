## The short answer

* A zoning application changes the rules for a specific property, such as height, density or permitted use, not the rules for the whole neighbourhood.
* Markham requires a Pre-Application Consultation Request and a public meeting before Council votes, so there are built-in points where residents can weigh in.
* You can check active applications through the city's ePLAN portal, ward councillor pages or by asking your agent to watch a specific address.
* The statutory target is 90 days for a zoning amendment alone and 120 days for a combined Official Plan and zoning amendment, but appeals to the Ontario Land Tribunal can stretch this well past a year.
* The effect on your property value depends on what gets approved and in most cases buyers and appraisers wait for a decision rather than pricing in a pending application.

## You buy a house and two years later there's a sign on the field next door

This happens more often than people expect in Markham. A family buys near an open field, a strip plaza or a low-rise block and a couple of years later a sign appears announcing a proposal for something much larger: a mid-rise building, a mixed-use development or a density far beyond what sits there now.

The first reaction is usually alarm and that's fair. Nobody budgets for a construction project next door when they buy. But a sign on a fence is the start of a process, not a done deal. Understanding how a Markham zoning application actually moves through the system takes a lot of the uncertainty out of it, even if it does not remove it entirely.

## What a Markham zoning application actually changes

Every property in Markham sits under two layers of planning rules: the Official Plan, which sets broad land use policy and the Zoning By-law, which sets the specific rules for a lot, like permitted use, height, setbacks and lot coverage. Markham's current zoning rules are consolidated under Comprehensive Zoning By-law 2024-19, which came into force in mid-2024 and replaced most of the older zoning across the city.

When a developer wants to build something the current zoning does not allow, such as a taller building, a different use or more units than the lot permits, they have to apply for an amendment. A Zoning By-law Amendment changes the site-specific rules. An Official Plan Amendment changes the underlying policy the zoning is built on. Markham allows both to be filed together as one combined Planning Project application when a proposal conflicts with both documents.

### Markham rezoning process, step by step

Before any application reaches City Council, Markham requires two gating steps. First, the applicant submits a Pre-Application Consultation Request through the city's ePLAN portal, where planning staff and any relevant agencies review the proposal and confirm what studies and documents will be required. Second, once those materials are ready, the applicant files the full application, which then goes through a prescreen review to confirm it is complete and the fees are paid.

Only once an application passes that prescreen and is accepted as complete does the formal review clock start. From there, Markham targets a 90-day Council decision for a zoning amendment on its own and 120 days when it is combined with an Official Plan Amendment. Those are Planning Act targets, not guarantees and larger or more contested proposals regularly run longer.

## Who else gets a say in a Markham planning application

Depending on where the property sits, more than just the city is involved. York Region co-approves all Official Plan Amendments, since Markham is a lower-tier municipality within the region. Properties in southern Markham may fall under the Toronto and Region Conservation Authority, while land near the Lake Simcoe watershed in the north falls under the Lake Simcoe Region Conservation Authority instead.

Markham also has a feature few other Ontario municipalities deal with: land near the Rouge River corridor can trigger review by Parks Canada, which has its own permitting authority over development near Rouge National Urban Park under federal legislation. That review runs on a separate track from the conservation authority process, with its own timeline and conditions, which is one more reason these applications can take longer than the headline figures suggest.

## How to check zoning in Markham for a specific address

If you own property near a vacant lot, a plaza or an older low-rise building, you do not have to wait for a sign to appear. Markham's online services let you search current zoning and look up active development and planning applications by address through the ePLAN system. Ward councillor pages, such as the active development application listings some wards maintain, often post plain-language summaries and meeting dates that are easier to follow than the formal city postings.

If you are actively watching an area, whether as a homeowner or someone thinking about buying there, it is worth checking these sources every few months, since applications can sit quietly for a long stretch and then move quickly once a public meeting is scheduled.

## Can you object to a Markham zoning application

Yes and the process gives you more than one opportunity. A statutory public meeting is required for Official Plan and Zoning By-law Amendment applications, where staff present the proposal and residents can ask questions or register to speak. You can also submit written comments ahead of time or fill out a deputation form to formally address a council committee.

If Council refuses an application or simply takes too long to decide, the developer can appeal to the Ontario Land Tribunal. Markham's own active application records show this happening regularly, including cases where an appeal was filed within weeks of a non-decision and a multi-day hearing was scheduled a year or more later. Once a matter reaches the Tribunal, the process becomes more formal and legal, but residents and ratepayer groups can still participate.

## Markham development applications and your property value

This is the question most owners actually want answered and the honest answer is that it depends heavily on what gets approved and how far along the process is. While an application is pending, most buyers and appraisers treat it as uncertain and do not adjust price much either way, since there is no guarantee it will be approved as proposed or approved at all.

Once a project is approved and under construction, the effect splits in both directions. A well-designed mixed-use development can bring amenities, transit investment and renewed retail that lift nearby values over time. A project that brings years of construction disruption, lost green space or a scale that clashes with the existing street can soften buyer interest in the short term. Neither outcome is guaranteed, which is exactly why it is worth tracking an application rather than guessing.

## What to do next

If you've spotted a sign, a notice or a mention of a project near your property, start by searching the address directly on Markham's ePLAN portal and checking your ward councillor's page for a plain-language summary. Mark the date of any upcoming public meeting and plan to attend, even just to listen, since the staff presentation usually explains the proposal more clearly than the notice itself.

If you are weighing a purchase near a site that looks likely to see future development, ask your agent to check for any active or recently closed planning applications on file before you make an offer, not after. And if an application is already moving through the system near your current home, keep a simple file of notices, meeting dates and any correspondence, since it makes it much easier to follow the process if it drags on or ends up at the Ontario Land Tribunal.
