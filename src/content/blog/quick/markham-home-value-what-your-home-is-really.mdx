## Why there is no single Markham home value

Markham covers a wide stretch of York Region and treating it as one market is where a lot of sellers go wrong. A detached home in Unionville, with its established tree-lined streets and the historic Main Street Unionville nearby, sells into a different buyer pool than a freehold townhouse in Cornell or a condo near Highway 7. Each of those areas has its own supply, its own typical buyer and its own price trend.

This is exactly why real estate boards track the MLS Home Price Index rather than relying on a simple average sale price. The HPI follows a "benchmark home", a property with typical features for its specific area, so you're comparing like with like over time instead of being thrown off by a month where more luxury homes happened to sell. If you're trying to find your home's market value, the benchmark for your specific neighbourhood and property type is a far better starting point than a city-wide figure.

## How to look up the market value of your home

There are a few ways people try to answer "how much is my home worth," and they are not equally reliable.

### Automated online estimators

These tools pull from public records and recent sales to spit out a number in seconds. They're useful for a very rough range, but they typically don't know about a finished basement, a recent kitchen renovation or a lot with an unusual shape. Treat the number as a starting conversation, not a figure to list your home at.

### MLS Home Price Index by neighbourhood

The HPI benchmark price, tracked by the Canadian Real Estate Association and updated through local boards, reflects a typical home for a specific area and property type. CREA reviews how the index is built every May to keep it current. This is a solid way to see the direction of prices in, say, Unionville specifically, rather than Markham as a whole.

### A comparative market analysis from a local agent

This is the most accurate free option and it's what actually determines a listing price. An agent pulls recent sold listings of homes similar to yours in size, age, condition and location, then adjusts for differences like a renovated bathroom or a busier street. Because it uses sales that have actually closed nearby, it accounts for the things an automated tool misses.

## What detached home prices in Unionville look like right now

Unionville consistently sits among Markham's stronger-performing areas thanks to its school catchments, mature streetscape and historic village core. Detached home prices there generally run above the Markham-wide median, though the exact gap moves month to month as inventory and buyer demand shift. Rather than quoting a fixed figure here, which would be out of date within weeks, the better approach is to check the current MLS HPI benchmark for Unionville detached homes or ask for a list of recent closed sales on comparable streets.

What's worth knowing is the pattern: in a market where overall Markham prices have cooled from their 2022 peak, established, amenity-rich pockets like Unionville tend to hold value better than newer or more commodity-type housing stock. That doesn't mean prices there are immune to broader conditions, it means the adjustment tends to be smaller.

## Understanding the current Markham real estate market

As of September 2026, the Markham real estate market showed a mix of signals. Sales were up slightly compared with the year before, while new listings and active inventory were both down, a combination that usually points toward tightening conditions. Despite that, the median sale price was down compared with a year earlier and the 12-month average price sat well below the peak recorded in August 2022.

Only about a third of new listings sold in that month and homes took roughly 24 days to sell on average, up from around 11 days the year before. Months of inventory were near 4.6, a level generally associated with conditions that favour buyers rather than sellers. In plain terms: there's more room for negotiation than there was a couple of years ago, but tightening supply suggests that could shift.

## How home type changes your Markham home value

Detached, semi-detached, townhouse and condo apartment are tracked separately in the MLS HPI for good reason: they sell to different buyers, at different price points and often move at different speeds. A detached home is a standalone, separately titled property. A semi-detached shares one wall with a neighbouring unit, with each half separately titled. A townhouse usually comes with its own land but may share walls and typically carries a common-area fee, putting it between a semi-detached home and a condo in both structure and price.

## What affects how long your home sits on the market

Days on market tells you how competitive your pricing and presentation are relative to current demand. Markham's average climbed from roughly 11 days to about 24 days year over year as of fall 2026, which reflects a cooling in buyer urgency rather than anything about any one listing. Still, within that average, well-priced homes in strong school zones or walkable village areas typically sell faster than homes that are priced against last year's market instead of this one.

A few things consistently affect time on market in any Markham neighbourhood:

* Pricing relative to recent comparable sales, not last year's peak.
* Condition and presentation, including basic staging and repairs before listing.
* How the listing is marketed and whether it reaches the right buyer pool for that home type.
* Broader supply in that specific area and price bracket, since a glut of similar listings slows everything down.
