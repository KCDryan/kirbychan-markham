## The short answer

* Downtown Markham is a planned district of condos, retail and offices built over the past fifteen or so years, not a single building or condo tower.
* Most housing stock here is condo apartments and some townhomes, with very few detached houses inside the core itself.
* The area sits close to Highway 404 and the 407 and is served by York Region Transit (YRT) buses, though it is not on a subway or GO train line directly.
* Buyers should budget for condo maintenance fees, which vary by building age and amenities, in addition to the purchase price.
* It is distinct from nearby Unionville, which has an older main street feel and more detached housing.

## What kind of neighbourhood is Downtown Markham?

Downtown Markham was designed from the ground up as a walkable, mixed-use district rather than growing organically like an older town centre. The plan brought together condo towers, low-rise retail, office buildings and public space around a central square, with the goal of giving York Region a dense, urban core of its own. If you have visited in the last decade you have likely seen new buildings go up nearly every year, because the area is still filling in.

That means living in Downtown Markham today feels different depending on which block you are on. Some streets are fully built out with shops at street level and condos above, busy with foot traffic most evenings. Other blocks are still construction sites or surface parking waiting for the next phase. Anyone buying here should expect ongoing development for a while yet, which is worth factoring into how a unit's view or noise level might change.

## Housing types and what buyers should expect

The housing stock in Downtown Markham is almost entirely condominiums, ranging from smaller mid-rise buildings to taller towers with a full slate of amenities. A handful of condo townhomes exist along the edges of the district, but detached and semi-detached houses are rare inside the core itself. If a private yard or a driveway matters to you, you will likely need to look at surrounding neighbourhoods like Unionville, Cornell or Berczy Village instead.

Unit sizes tend to run smaller than what buyers find in older, more established parts of Markham, with a mix of studios, one-bedroom and two-bedroom layouts aimed at singles, couples and small families. Buildings vary quite a bit in age, so a unit from an earlier phase of the development may have different finishes, ceiling heights and mechanical systems than one from a more recent tower. Always ask for the building's age, the status certificate and the reserve fund study before making an offer, since these tell you far more about a condo's real condition than a fresh coat of paint in the lobby.

### Buying new construction vs resale in the area

Because the district is still growing, buyers regularly choose between a resale unit in an established building or a pre-construction unit in a project that has not broken ground yet. Resale gives you a finished space you can see and inspect now, along with a clearer sense of the building's actual maintenance fees and reserve fund health. Pre-construction ties up a deposit for a longer period and comes with occupancy timelines that can shift, so read the purchase agreement closely and understand the assignment and cancellation clauses before committing.

## Getting around: commute and transit options

One of the bigger draws of living in Downtown Markham is how close it sits to major highways. Highway 404 runs right along the district, connecting south to the 401 and north toward Newmarket, while the 407 crosses nearby for anyone heading east or west across the GTA. Drive times into downtown Toronto usually fall somewhere between 30 and 45 minutes outside rush hour and considerably longer during peak commuting times, so confirm current traffic patterns if your daily commute depends on it.

Transit-wise, York Region Transit (YRT) runs bus routes through the district connecting to GO Transit stations and, further afield, to the Toronto subway system. There is no subway station or GO train stop directly inside Downtown Markham itself, so most residents either drive, cycle short distances or take a bus to connect with a train line. If a car-free commute into Toronto's core is a hard requirement, it is worth mapping the exact route from a specific building before buying, since walk times to bus stops vary block by block.

## Amenities, shopping and daily life

Daily errands are easy to handle on foot in most of Downtown Markham. Grocery stores, a cinema, pharmacies, banks and a wide range of restaurants sit within a short walk of most condo buildings, along with parks and public squares used for community events through the warmer months. Many buildings also include their own amenities such as gyms, party rooms, guest suites and rooftop terraces, which factor into the monthly maintenance fee.

For bigger shopping trips or specialty stores, residents typically drive a short distance to nearby plazas or to Markville Mall. Families with children should also look at proximity to parks and community centres, since the core itself is denser and has less green space per resident than older parts of Markham.

## Schools and family considerations

Downtown Markham falls under the York Region District School Board for public education and the York Catholic District School Board for the Catholic system. Because school boundaries are drawn by exact address and can shift as new schools open to serve a growing population, confirm the specific school assigned to any address you are considering directly with the board rather than assuming based on a neighbouring building.

Families choosing a condo lifestyle here should also think about unit size and storage, since most layouts are built for one or two occupants rather than a larger household. Some buildings offer larger two- and three-bedroom units better suited to families, but they make up a smaller share of overall inventory.

## Costs to expect: price, fees and taxes

Condo prices in Downtown Markham vary by unit size, floor, view, building age and current market conditions, so any specific figure quoted here would go stale quickly. The more useful approach is to pull recent comparable sales for the exact building or a very similar one nearby, which I can put together for you, rather than relying on a general market average.

Beyond the purchase price, budget for monthly condo maintenance fees (which cover building upkeep, amenities and often heat or water), property tax billed by the City of Markham and York Region and closing costs that include legal fees and, for many buyers, Ontario's land transfer tax. First-time buyers should also check whether they qualify for any land transfer tax rebate, since eligibility rules and rebate amounts are set by the province and can change.

## What Downtown Markham offers and what it lacks

Downtown Markham is mostly condo apartments and townhomes, so living here means a low-maintenance home close to work, shops and highways, without a yard or a large house to look after. Its location near offices and a university campus is also why some investors look here for rentals.

What it offers less of is private outdoor space, backyards and the mature, tree-lined streets of older parts of the city. If those matter to you, compare it with Unionville, Cornell or Berczy Village, all still within Markham.
