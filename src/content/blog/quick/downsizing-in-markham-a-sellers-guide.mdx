## The short answer

* Downsizing works best when it is planned six to twelve months ahead, not decided the week you list.
* Your equity, not just your new mortgage payment, determines what kind of home you can move into comfortably.
* Selling first gives certainty on budget, buying first gives a smoother move and both have real trade offs.
* Condos and smaller freehold homes in Markham each solve different problems, so match the choice to your lifestyle, not just the price.
* Decluttering and sorting belongings early saves weeks of stress compared to doing it in the final month.

## Why Markham Sellers Are Downsizing Right Now

A lot of the calls I get about downsizing in Markham come from the same place: a home that made sense fifteen or twenty years ago now feels like too much. Kids have moved out, the stairs are less appealing than they used to be and the yard work that once felt satisfying now feels like a chore that eats a Saturday.

There is also the financial side. Markham homeowners who bought fifteen, twenty or thirty years ago have often built up significant equity. Selling and moving into something smaller can free up a meaningful amount of cash for retirement, travel or simply reducing monthly costs. That is a real, practical reason on top of the lifestyle one.

None of this means downsizing is automatically the right move for everyone. Some people genuinely love their space and their neighbourhood and are happy to manage the upkeep. The point of this guide is to help you figure out, with real numbers rather than guesses, whether it makes sense for you.

## Signs It Might Be Time to Downsize

There is no set age or rule that says you should downsize. But a few patterns show up often enough among Markham sellers that they are worth mentioning.

* You are heating, cooling and maintaining rooms you rarely use.
* Property taxes and utility costs are taking a bigger bite out of your budget than they used to.
* Home maintenance, from the roof to the furnace, is starting to feel like a burden rather than routine upkeep.
* You would rather spend your time and money on travel, hobbies or family than on a bigger home.
* Stairs, a large lot or an isolated location are becoming harder to manage day to day.

If two or three of these sound familiar, it is worth at least running the numbers. That does not mean listing your home tomorrow, it means understanding what your options actually look like.

## What Your Home Is Worth vs. What You Need to Buy

The math behind downsizing your home in Markham comes down to one simple comparison: what your current home will sell for, minus selling costs, versus what a smaller home or condo will cost you to buy, plus buying costs.

Selling costs typically include real estate commission, legal fees and any repairs or staging done before listing. Buying costs include Ontario's land transfer tax, legal fees on the purchase side and moving expenses. Your lawyer or agent can walk you through the specific figures for your situation, since these vary depending on price and property type.

What often surprises sellers is how much equity is left over after both transactions. In many cases, downsizing from a larger detached home into a townhouse or condo in the same general area frees up a substantial amount of cash, even after all costs are accounted for. I always recommend getting a written comparative market analysis on your current home before making any decisions, rather than relying on what a neighbour's house sold for or what an online estimate suggests.

## Sell First or Buy First?

This is one of the most common questions sellers ask me and there is no single right answer. It depends on your risk tolerance, your finances and how flexible you can be about timing.

### Selling First

Selling your current home before buying the next one gives you certainty. You know exactly how much money you have to work with and you are not carrying two mortgages or two sets of property taxes at once. The trade off is that you may need to arrange temporary housing if you have not found your next home yet or negotiate a longer closing period with your buyer.

### Buying First

Buying your next home before selling avoids the disruption of a temporary move. You pack once and move once. The risk is financial: if your current home takes longer than expected to sell or sells for less than hoped, you could be carrying two properties at once. A bridge loan can help cover that gap for qualified buyers, but it comes with its own costs and is not something every lender offers.

Many Markham sellers downsizing within the same area choose a hybrid approach: list their current home with a longer closing date and use that window to shop for the next place. It is not always possible, but when it works, it removes a lot of the stress.

## Condo, Townhouse or Smaller House?

Once you have a sense of your budget, the next decision is what kind of home to move into. Each option solves a different problem.

| Option                                  | Best for                                             | Watch out for                                             |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Condo apartment                         | Low maintenance, amenities, lock-and-leave lifestyle | Monthly condo fees, corporation rules, less storage       |
| Townhouse                               | A middle ground between space and upkeep             | Still some exterior maintenance, shared walls             |
| Smaller detached house                  | Keeping independence and a yard, just less of it     | You still handle all maintenance yourself                 |
| Retirement or adult lifestyle community | Social environment, age-specific amenities           | Fees, rules and sometimes buy-in costs to check carefully |

There is no universally right answer here. Some sellers want to fully hand off maintenance and love condo living. Others want to keep a small garden and some independence and a townhouse or smaller freehold suits them better. Being honest with yourself about how much hands-off living actually appeals to you, versus how much space and privacy you want to keep, will point you toward the right choice faster than comparing price tags alone.

## Getting Your Home Ready to Sell

Downsizing usually means dealing with decades of belongings, which is often the most emotionally and physically demanding part of the whole process. Starting early makes a real difference.

* Begin decluttering room by room, three to six months before you plan to list if possible.
* Sort belongings into keep, sell, donate and give to family, rather than trying to decide item by item under time pressure.
* Get a pre-listing home inspection so there are no surprises once you are under contract.
* Talk to your agent about which repairs or updates are worth doing for your specific street and price point and which are not worth the money.

Markham has consignment shops, auction houses for higher value items and several donation centres that make this part easier. I can point you toward the ones that tend to work well depending on what you are dealing with.

## Tax and Legal Things to Confirm Before You List

Most Markham homeowners selling their principal residence will not owe capital gains tax on the sale, thanks to the principal residence exemption. But there are exceptions, particularly if you owned a second property at the same time, rented out part of your home or used part of it for business purposes.

This is not something to guess about. Before you list, have a conversation with an accountant who can confirm exactly how the sale will be treated in your situation. It is a short conversation that can prevent an unpleasant surprise at tax time.

On the buying side, Ontario's land transfer tax applies to your new purchase, calculated on a sliding scale based on price. Markham does not have an additional municipal land transfer tax the way Toronto does, which is one small advantage for buyers moving within York Region. Your lawyer will confirm the exact amount once you have a purchase price in mind.
