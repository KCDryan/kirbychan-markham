## The short answer

* Markham is an incorporated city with its own mayor, council and municipal services, covering roughly 211 square kilometres of York Region.
* Thornhill is a historic community split by Yonge Street, with the east side in Markham and the west side in Vaughan.
* Neither Markham nor Thornhill is part of the City of Toronto, though both border it and sit within the Greater Toronto Area.
* Property taxes, school boundaries and zoning rules differ depending on which side of Yonge Street a Thornhill property falls on.
* Markham is roughly 25 to 35 kilometres from downtown Toronto, with drive times that swing a lot depending on traffic and time of day.

## The Difference Between Markham and Thornhill: Two Different Kinds of Place

People often ask about Markham vs Thornhill as if they are choosing between two separate cities. That's not quite the comparison. Markham is a city. Thornhill is a community and a split one at that. Comparing them head to head is a bit like comparing a province to a neighbourhood that happens to straddle a provincial line.

Markham became a city in 2012, after decades as a town. It has its own council, its own mayor and distinct neighbourhoods such as Unionville, Cornell, Milliken, Berczy Village and Buttonville, each with a different feel and a different price point. Thornhill is one of the older communities that sits partly inside Markham's eastern boundary and partly inside Vaughan's.

## Is Thornhill part of Markham or Vaughan?

Both, depending on which side of Yonge Street you are standing on. Yonge Street runs north to south and acts as the dividing line. East of Yonge Street, Thornhill is governed by the City of Markham. West of Yonge Street, it is governed by the City of Vaughan.

This split goes back to how York County was reorganized. Thornhill was settled as a single village in the 1790s, grew into a Police Village by 1931 and when York Region was formed in 1971, the community was divided between the newly formed towns of Markham and Vaughan rather than becoming its own municipality. That division has stuck ever since, through Markham's shift to city status in 2012 and Vaughan's in 2001.

So if someone asks is Thornhill part of Markham, the honest answer is: part of it is. The other part answers to Vaughan. Same name, same general area, two different town halls.

## Is Thornhill part of Markham or Vaughan for everyday matters?

This isn't just a line on a map, it affects practical things buyers and owners deal with regularly:

* **Property taxes:** set and collected by whichever city your side falls under, so a home on the Markham side and one a few streets away on the Vaughan side can have different tax rates. Always confirm the current rate with the relevant city rather than assuming it matches a neighbour's bill.
* **School boards and boundaries:** families on the Markham side are assigned to schools through different boundary maps than families on the Vaughan side, even if the schools sit close together.
* **Building permits and zoning:** renovations, additions and new construction go through Markham's planning department on one side and Vaughan's on the other.
* **Garbage collection, by-laws and local council representation:** all run separately depending on which city you're in.

## Heritage and character: why Thornhill feels distinct

Thornhill has a genuinely old core, which is part of why it reads as its own place even though it sits inside two cities. The village grew up around a gristmill, sawmill and tannery built in the 1820s and the historic stretch of John Street still has a cluster of Victorian, Queen Anne, Georgian and Edwardian era buildings. The Markham side of this historic core is protected under a Heritage Conservation District, similar in spirit to the protections around Unionville's historic main street.

That heritage character is one reason Thornhill listings often get marketed on their own, separate from "Markham" or "Vaughan" more broadly. Buyers searching for a Thornhill home are usually drawn to the mature trees, established streets and older housing stock rather than searching for a specific city name. It's worth knowing, though, that "Thornhill" on its own doesn't tell you which city's rules apply to the specific property you're looking at.

## Is Markham part of Toronto?

No. Markham is its own city, with its own government, inside York Region. It borders the City of Toronto along Steeles Avenue, which is why people sometimes talk about Markham as if it blends into Toronto, but municipally it's separate. The same goes for Thornhill: neither side, Markham or Vaughan, falls inside Toronto's city limits.

This matters for buyers coming from outside the region. Land transfer tax, municipal tax rates and some by-laws differ between Toronto and the surrounding 905 cities, so it's worth confirming which municipality actually applies to a property before assuming Toronto rules apply.

## How far is Markham from downtown Toronto?

Most of Markham sits somewhere between 25 and 35 kilometres from downtown Toronto, depending on which part of Markham you're measuring from. The southern parts of Markham, closer to Steeles Avenue, are naturally closer than the northern parts up toward Stouffville Road.

Drive time is a better practical measure than distance for most buyers and it swings a lot. Outside rush hour, expect somewhere around 30 to 45 minutes via Highway 404 or 407. During peak commute times, that can stretch well past an hour. GO Transit's Richmond Hill and Stouffville lines, along with the Yonge subway extension connections, give commuters an alternative that's often more predictable than driving.
