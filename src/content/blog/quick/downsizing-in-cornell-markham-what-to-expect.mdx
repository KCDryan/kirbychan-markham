Cornell is unusual in Markham, because downsizing here does not have to mean leaving.

## The neighbourhood does the work

Cornell is a planned community in east Markham built on a grid, with front porches, rear lane garages and Markham Stouffville Hospital at its edge. The housing mix is the point: freehold townhomes, semis and detached homes on the grid, plus newer condo stock. Our [Cornell neighbourhood guide](/cornell-markham/) sets out the layout, the transit and who tends to buy here.

For a downsizer that mix is a real advantage. In much of Markham, moving from a house to an apartment means changing neighbourhood as well as home. In Cornell you can often stay inside the same community, keep your doctors and your routines and stay close to the hospital.

## What still needs planning

The sale and the purchase are still two transactions that have to fit together.

- **Timing.** Two closings on the same day sounds tidy and leaves no room for anything to slip. Decide early how much overlap you want and ask a mortgage professional about bridge financing before you need it.
- **Condo fees and rules.** If you are moving into a condo townhome or an apartment, read what the fees cover and what the rules say about pets, parking and visitors. A lawyer reviewing the status certificate is the step that catches problems.
- **Contents.** Decades in a family home take longer to sort than anyone expects. Start before you list, room by room.
- **Presentation.** Cornell buyers are comparing your home with others on the same grid, so condition and staging carry more weight than they would in a neighbourhood with more varied stock.

## A sensible order of work

1. Decide the destination: staying in Cornell or moving to [Unionville](/unionville-markham/), [Downtown Markham](/downtown-markham/) or somewhere else entirely.
2. Get a current read on what your home is worth.
3. Map the two closings and the money between them.
4. Start the decluttering.
5. Prepare the house for sale.
