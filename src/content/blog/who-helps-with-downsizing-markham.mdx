## The short answer

Ask who helps with downsizing in Markham and you will get a list of companies that do very different things. Some sell houses. Some pack them. Some empty them. Some handle the tax and the title. All of them are legitimately "downsizing help" and the confusion about which is which costs families weeks.

Here is the map. Our [Markham downsizing guide](/downsizing-markham/) covers the costs, the destinations and the sequencing. This post is about the people.

## The real estate agent: the two transactions and the gap between them

The agent's job is the sale of the home you are leaving, the purchase of the one you are going to and, above all, the sequencing of the two closings so you are never carrying two properties or standing in the driveway with nowhere to go.

That last part is the skill that matters most in a downsizing move and it is invisible in a listing. It means negotiating a longer closing on the sale to give you time to buy or a shorter one on the purchase to avoid bridge financing. It also means knowing which lever to pull for your situation. Our post on [sell first or buy first](/blog/sell-first-or-buy-first-downsizing-markham/) works through that decision.

A downsizing agent should also be honest about price, because a home held for thirty years is often priced on what the family needs rather than on what comparable sales support. That is how a house sits. A [free home valuation](/home-valuation/) is where that conversation starts.

The agent does not pack, sort or move anything. Some will tell you they do. What they mean is that they know someone who does.

## The senior move manager: the contents

This is the profession most people have not heard of until they need it. It is most of what came up when we looked at who is recommended for downsizing help in Markham.

A senior move manager handles the physical and emotional side of a later life move: working through the house room by room to decide what goes, what stays and what is passed on; packing; coordinating movers, donations and disposal; and then unpacking and setting up the new home so it is livable on the first night rather than a month later. Many belong to the National Association of Senior and Specialty Move Managers, which describes itself as the leading body for professionals who help older adults and families organise, declutter, downsize, relocate or age in place.

Whether you need one depends on the house. A home with a normal amount of contents can be managed by family and a good mover. A home with a lifetime in it usually cannot and the contents are the stage that most often decides how long the whole move takes. Our post on [how long downsizing takes](/blog/how-long-downsizing-takes-markham/) sets out why. A move manager earns their fee in the weeks they save and in the arguments they prevent.

They do not sell the house.

## The estate clearing company: what is left

Once the family has taken what it wants, something has to happen to the rest. Estate clearing companies sort what remains, sell what has value through a contents or estate sale, donate what can be donated and clear the rest so the house can be listed empty.

They are used in downsizing and after a death and the two overlap: a family clearing a parent's home before sale needs exactly this. Our guide to [selling an estate home in Markham](/selling-an-estate-home-markham/) covers the probate side of that situation.

Some move managers offer clearing as well and some clearing companies offer moving. Ask before you hire two firms for one job.

## The lawyer and the accountant: before anyone else

These two are usually hired last and should be hired first, because their answers shape everything the others do.

A **real estate lawyer** completes both transactions, registers the transfer and handles the land transfer tax on the purchase. If you are buying a condo, they review the status certificate, which the corporation must deliver within 10 days of the request for a fee capped at $100 including taxes. A move is also a sensible moment to have powers of attorney and a will reviewed.

An **accountant** confirms the tax position before you sell. The Canada Revenue Agency's principal residence exemption can eliminate the capital gain on a home that has been your principal residence for every year you owned it, but the sale still has to be reported and the calculation changes if you or a spouse ever owned a second property. If you are 65 or older and have been deferring property tax under the City of Markham's seniors programme, that deferred amount comes due when the property is transferred. It belongs in what you expect to net.

## The order that works

1. **Lawyer and accountant.** Tax, title, powers of attorney. Their answers set the budget.
2. **Agent.** Destination, valuation, timeline, sell first or buy first.
3. **Move manager or clearing company.** Started months before listing, not after the sale is firm.
4. **Movers.** Booked once the closing dates are set.

Families who hire in that order move on their own schedule. Families who hire the agent first and think about the contents last move on a buyer's schedule, which is how sorting a lifetime ends up crammed into the final three weeks before closing.

## Questions to ask each one

**The agent:** How would you sequence my sale and purchase? What would you do if my sale takes three months longer than expected? What do comparable sales in my part of Markham actually support?

**The move manager:** Are you a member of a professional association? What is included and what is billed separately? Who is physically in my house on the day?

**The clearing company:** How do you value contents? What happens to what does not sell? Do you insure what you handle?

**The lawyer:** Do you handle downsizing moves regularly? Will you review the status certificate before the condition expires? Should my powers of attorney be updated while we are at it?
