## The short answer

Buying a house with a pool in Markham means taking on a set of City rules along with the backyard. Markham's Pool Enclosure By-law 2015-96 says every owner of a pool must put up and maintain an enclosure around the entire pool area so the pool is not readily accessible. Those duties pass to you on closing day.

For many families moving up from a townhouse to a detached home, a pool is part of the appeal. It is also a structure with its own permit, its own fence standard and its own maintenance rules. This post explains what the by-law says, how the City's pool enclosure permit works and what to check on a pool you can only see under a winter cover.

This is general information taken from City of Markham pages and the by-law itself. It is not legal advice. Your real estate lawyer should review the specific property and the wording of your offer.

## What counts as a pool under Markham's by-law

The definition is wider than most buyers expect. The by-law defines a pool as any body of water outdoors on private property, held in by artificial means, where the water can be more than 0.6 metres (24 inches) deep at any point. It names these as included:

- in-ground swimming pools
- above-ground swimming pools
- inflatable pools
- hot tubs, spas and whirlpools
- wading pools
- landscaped ponds

Stormwater management facilities and natural bodies of water are excluded. The practical point for a buyer is that a deep ornamental pond or a backyard hot tub can bring the same obligations as a full swimming pool.

The by-law's definition of "owner" also goes beyond the name on title. It includes a tenant or occupant who has care or control of the property. If you plan to rent the home out, the tenant shares the duty to keep the enclosure in order.

## Markham pool fence and gate rules at a glance

Schedule A of the by-law sets the basic measurements for every pool enclosure in Markham:

| Rule in Schedule A | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum height of fences and gates | 1.2 metres (4 feet) |
| Maximum height of fences and gates | 1.8 metres (6 feet) |
| Minimum distance from the edge of the water | 1.2 metres (4 feet) |
| Maximum gap between the bottom of the fence or gate and the ground | 100 millimetres (4 inches) |

The body of the by-law adds more rules that you can check with your own eyes during a showing:

- Height is measured from grade on the outside of the enclosure, at every point along it.
- No vertical or horizontal opening in the enclosure may be wider than 100 millimetres (4 inches).
- There must be no rails or bracing on the outside that would help a child climb.
- No barbed wire, sharp projections or electric fencing may form part of it.
- Enclosures must be built of wood, metal or chain link, with further schedules for each material.
- Nothing may be piled or leaned against the enclosure in a way that makes it easier to climb.
- The enclosure belongs in the rear yard unless the City's by-law manager approves another location.

### Gates and doors

Gates get the most attention in the by-law. Every gate needs a self-closing device that returns it to the closed position within 30 seconds and a lockable self-latching device on the inside face near the top. Metal gates also need a covering plate at least 0.3 metres by 0.3 metres so the latch cannot be reached from outside.

Where double gates are used, one gate must have the self-closing and self-latching devices and the other must be fixed to the ground or another solid object so it cannot be opened without releasing it first.

Doors that lead straight from a building into the pool area, including doors from an attached or detached garage, need a bolt latch or chain latch at least 5 feet 6 inches above the inside floor. The by-law excludes doors located in a dwelling unit from that rule.

Finally, the by-law says gates must be kept locked with a padlock when the pool is not in active use. A separate section says access points must stay latched and padlocked at all times unless a responsible person is present and supervising inside the enclosed area.

## Hot tubs and above-ground pools in Markham

Two partial exemptions matter for buyers.

A hot tub does not need an enclosure if it has a substantial cover that can hold 90.72 kilograms (200 pounds) and the cover is fitted and locked when the tub is not in use. If the house you are buying comes with a hot tub, check that the cover is in good condition and actually locks. If it does not, the enclosure rules apply.

An above-ground pool can skip the standard fence and gate rules if all of the following are true:

1. The pool's outside walls plus any guard on top are between 1.5 metres (5 feet) and 2 metres (6 feet 6 inches) above finished grade.
2. The outside walls and guard are built so they do not help anyone climb.
3. The outside of the pool is at least 1.2 metres (4 feet) from every lot line. The zoning by-law can require a larger distance.
4. The entrance ladder is enclosed by a fence and gate that meet the standard rules.

If any of these is missing, the pool needs a full enclosure like an in-ground pool.

## How the pool enclosure permit works in Markham

The by-law says no one may build or install a pool until plans have been submitted to the Chief Building Official and a permit has been issued. It also says water may not be put in a pool until the enclosure has been inspected and approved.

The City's [building permit process page](https://www.markham.ca/economic-development-business/building-permits/building-permit-process) lists installing or changing a swimming pool fence enclosure among the projects that need a permit. The same page says fences other than a pool enclosure fence do not. Pool enclosure permits are submitted online through ePLAN. The City says paper applications are no longer accepted.

The City's ePLAN quick reference for pool enclosures, dated May 2023, says the permit is for the fencing that protects the pool and is required before an in-ground or above-ground pool is built on a residential property. It lists what an application must include:

- a site plan showing the existing buildings and the pool, the fence height, material and location, the clearance between fence and pool and the location of the gates
- the location of any septic system and its clearance from the pool, for properties on a private sewage system
- a Swimming Pool Enclosure application form and the Applicable Law Checklist
- a Swimming Pool Enclosure Letter of Undertaking
- a Swimming Pool Utilities Clearance stamped approved by Alectra

The building permit process page gives a target of 10 business days for the first review of a pool enclosure permit, whether the application is complete or incomplete.

Why this matters to a buyer: if you plan to replace an old fence, move a gate or add a pool after you move in, you will go through this process. If the current owner changed the enclosure, ask whether they did.

## Buying a house with a pool in Markham: what to check before the offer is firm

A pool is easy to admire in July. If you are viewing homes between now and spring, the pool will probably be closed for the season and under a cover. A practical order for a buyer:

1. Ask the seller, through the agents, for the pool enclosure permit and any inspection approval, plus records of the pool's installation, liner or finish, heater and pump.
2. Check the City's permit records for the address. Our post on [selling a house with an open permit in Markham](/blog/selling-house-open-permit-markham/) explains the City's ePLAN search and the Building Standards service requests that go with it.
3. Walk the whole fence line. Check the height from the outside, the gaps between boards, the space under the fence and anything stacked against it.
4. Open every gate and let go. It should swing shut and latch on its own. Look for the latch on the inside near the top and a covering plate on metal gates.
5. Look at every door from the house or garage that opens straight into the pool area and check for a high bolt or chain latch.
6. Check the hot tub cover, if there is one, for condition and a working lock.
7. Look at the pool cover. The by-law says no owner may allow standing water in a pool or on top of a pool cover, so a cover holding a pond of rainwater is something to ask about.
8. Ask your home inspector what they can and cannot assess on a closed pool. Our guide to [home inspections for Ontario buyers](/blog/home-inspection-ontario-buyers/) covers what an inspection condition does.

Anything you cannot check in winter is a question for your lawyer and your agent. Our post on [conditions in an offer in Ontario](/blog/conditions-in-an-offer-ontario/) explains how conditions work in general. How a particular pool should be handled in your offer is something to settle with your own lawyer.

It is also worth telling your insurer about the pool before closing. Our guide to [home insurance for buyers in Markham](/blog/home-insurance-first-time-buyers-markham/) explains what to ask.

## Owning a pool in Markham after you move in

The by-law keeps applying long after closing. Three rules catch new owners most often.

### Repairs and work orders

No owner may let a pool or enclosure fall into disrepair, which the by-law describes as dilapidated, broken, missing parts or built from materials or in a way the by-law does not allow. The City can serve a work order. If the work is not done in time, the City can do it and add the cost to the property tax roll.

### Draining and lowering the water

The by-law says pool water must not be discharged so that it flows onto a neighbouring property, flows into a valley or ravine or causes erosion of a ravine slope. Water may only go onto City property in four ways:

| Allowed discharge under the by-law | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| By hose to the street | Directly to the street in front of your own property |
| Into a catch basin on your property | The catch basin must be on the same property as the pool |
| Into a neighbour's catch basin | Only with the neighbour's consent |
| Onto your driveway or front yard | Of the property where the pool is located |

Any debris or soil left on the road must be cleaned up right away. The valley and ravine rule matters most if the lot you are buying backs onto one.

### Grading and new fences

Installing a pool or enclosure must not change the property's existing grades or drainage pattern unless the permit allows it. And if you put up a new fence next to an existing pool enclosure, the by-law says that fence must also meet the pool rules. Markham's separate fence by-law, 277-97, generally allows rear and side fences up to 1.8 metres, according to the City's by-law summary.

### Fines

The by-law sets fines of $200 to $50,000 on a first conviction and $400 to $100,000 on a later conviction for the same offence. A continuing offence can bring $200 to $10,000 for each day it continues. For questions, the City's summary page lists the By-law Enforcement and Licensing Department at 905-479-7782.

## Moving up to a Markham home with a pool: next steps

If a pool is on your list as you move up to a larger home in Unionville, Angus Glen, Markham Village or elsewhere in Markham, a short checklist:

- Read the City's Pool Enclosure By-law once before you start viewing homes.
- Ask for the enclosure permit and pool records early, ideally before you write an offer.
- Test every gate, latch and door that opens into the pool area during your showing.
- Have your lawyer review how the offer deals with a pool you cannot fully inspect in winter.
- Budget for fence or cover repairs if the enclosure does not meet the rules.
